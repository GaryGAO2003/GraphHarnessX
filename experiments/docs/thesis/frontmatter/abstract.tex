\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing\noindent
A large language model (LLM) agent is a model plus a harness, and a
growing literature evolves the harness automatically. HarnessX/AEGIS is a
published harness-level closed loop with released code and, to our
knowledge, the most heavily governed one: four meta-roles read every trajectory, successes
included, and each candidate edit must clear five deterministic gates.

We vendored it byte-for-byte and executed it at scale --- its first
independent execution: six campaigns of sixteen rounds, 8{,}393 fresh
evaluations on a 100-task text-only GAIA bed, exposing defects. Its evidence signal grades a tool
call as ``used'' only if the next assistant message repeats twenty
characters of its output verbatim, while this bed's answers have a median
length of nine: the column is blind by construction, applies its negative
verdict to $75.0\%$ of answer-carrying calls, and reaches $86.0\%$ of
dossiers. One candidate shipped on that column. Four feedback channels are
written but never delivered, the paper's ratchet is not implemented, and
the loop edits itself through free text, so nothing confirms that a
candidate document and the applied change are one intervention.

We built GraphHarnessX (GHX) in response: a typed executable composition
graph, a per-invocation execution DAG whose edges are observed rather than
reconstructed, causal cones over it, a replay gate and a sixth acceptance
gate that check a candidate's claim against the recorded execution, and a
typed candidate surface --- attached to the unmodified
substrate from outside, flags off. What it buys is
checkability: the false column reaches 0 of 859 graph-arm dossiers,
malformed candidate deaths halve, and eighteen candidates that had cleared
all five official gates were caught naming a nonexistent node.

The pre-specified hypothesis --- that faithful execution-level evidence
improves edit localization and outcomes --- is not supported on declared
substitute endpoints, because the original one proved not measurable;
\emph{that} is the thesis's result. Same-configuration re-measurement flips $21.0$,
$20.1$ and $19.2\%$ of task outcomes across three seeds, so total-score
deltas of five tasks or fewer carry no information. Our archives show
the unmodified loop had already prescribed the same repairs correctly,
four campaigns earlier, from single observations and false evidence:
prescription was never the bottleneck; a pre-specified efficacy trial
returned zero. A positive control shows the score
axis recovered the one known-effective intervention, because
its effect was three times the per-edit effects this loop produces;
two campaign-wide sweeps show that clearing the noise band is neither
necessary nor sufficient for an edit's success.

We state the consequence as a proposition --- self-evolution is variation
plus selection plus \emph{readout}, and, on this bed and system class,
below-floor selection makes cumulative gain non-positive regardless of
variation quality --- and offer a four-rung readout ladder on which every surveyed system admits
candidates at rung zero. Rungs 0 to 2 are live in GHX; rung 3 --- did the
edit change outcomes --- is the field's missing organ.

The graph did not make the loop smarter. It made the loop checkable.
\end{abstract}

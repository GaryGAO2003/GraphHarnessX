\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing\noindent
A large language model (LLM) agent is a model plus a harness, and a
growing literature evolves the harness automatically. HarnessX/AEGIS is a
published harness-level closed loop with released code: four meta-roles
read every trajectory, and each candidate edit must clear five
deterministic gates.

We vendored it byte-for-byte and ran it --- an independent
execution: six campaigns of sixteen rounds, 8{,}393 fresh
evaluations on a 100-task text-only GAIA bed. Its evidence signal grades a tool
call as ``used'' only if the next assistant message repeats twenty
characters of its output verbatim, while this bed's answers have a median
length of nine: the column is blind by construction, applies its negative
verdict to $75.0\%$ of answer-carrying calls, and reaches $86.0\%$ of
dossiers. One candidate shipped on that column. Four feedback channels are
written but never delivered, the paper's ratchet is absent, and the loop
edits itself through free text, so nothing confirms a candidate document
and the applied change are one intervention.

We built GraphHarnessX (GHX) in response. It re-expresses the harness as a
typed executable composition graph that drives execution at parity with the
legacy runloop, and adds a per-invocation execution DAG whose edges are
observed, not reconstructed, causal cones on it, a replay gate and a sixth
acceptance gate that check a candidate's claim against the record, and a
candidate surface of typed graph edits --- all attached from outside, flags
off. What it buys is checkability: the false column reaches 0 of 859 graph-arm dossiers,
malformed candidate deaths halve, and eighteen candidates that had passed
all five official gates, pooled over three graph seeds, named a node with
no execution record.

The original localization endpoint of the pre-specified hypothesis proved
unmeasurable. Substitute endpoints and the whole-stack two-arm comparison
are descriptive; a separately protocolled bundle trial detected no
improvement and returned a negative point estimate. Same-configuration
re-measurement flips $21.0$, $20.1$ and $19.2\%$ of task outcomes across
three seeds, so small total-score deltas must be read against that
variation. The archives show the unmodified loop had already prescribed
the same repairs, four campaigns earlier, from single observations and a
construction-mismatched evidence column: prescription was never the
bottleneck. One post-hoc positive-control event shows that the score axis
recovered a large known-effective intervention; it does not calibrate
detection of smaller effects.

We state the consequence as a proposition --- self-evolution is variation
plus selection plus \emph{readout}, and on this bed and system class
score-based retention may mis-rank effects small relative to readout
variation. Non-positive cumulative gain is a possible dynamic, not one
estimated here --- and offer a four-rung readout ladder on which every
surveyed system admits candidates at rung R0. R0 to R2 are live in GHX; R3 --- did the edit
change outcomes --- exists here only as a hand-run efficacy-trial protocol,
wired into no loop in the field.
\end{abstract}

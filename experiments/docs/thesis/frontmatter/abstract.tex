\begin{abstract}
\singlespacing\noindent
HarnessX/AEGIS is a
published harness-level closed loop with released code: four meta-roles,
the first of which reads every trajectory, and five deterministic gates
that each candidate edit must clear.

We vendored the released single-lineage implementation byte-for-byte; distinct from
the published ensemble result, the study runs no-graph executions of that release and
GHX whole-stack campaigns: six campaigns of sixteen rounds, 8{,}276 fresh evaluations on a
100-task text-only GAIA bed. The evidence signal calls output ``used'' only when the next message
repeats twenty characters verbatim; it cannot credit reuse confined to shorter
spans. Among answer-carrying calls its $75.0\%$ negative-verdict rate is a rule
output, not a verified false-negative rate, and it reaches $86.0\%$ of available dossiers. Several feedback paths fail to inform subsequent decisions,
and the score clamp fired five times, three inside the same-configuration
band; the paper's ratchet is absent, and the loop edits itself through free
text, so nothing confirms a candidate document and the applied change are
one intervention.

We built GraphHarnessX (GHX) in response. Linked graphs represent harness
composition and observed execution: one exporter materializes released
hooks, processors and slots, the other records runtime tool calls and
nested-agent events per invocation. Causal cones, replay and a sixth
acceptance gate check claims against that record; typed candidate operations
cover graph and tool-registry changes --- all attached from outside, flags
off by default. Reported observations: the construction-mismatched column reaches 0 of 859 dossiers
in the whitelist graph campaign, the share rejected as malformed at the recorded gates halves, and eighteen candidates that had passed all five official gates, pooled over
three graph seeds, named a target absent or, for a processor, non-intervening
in the one selected failure replayed.

The evidence, in order. Same-configuration re-measurement flips $21.0$,
$20.1$ and $19.2\%$ of task outcomes across three seeds; every score
reading below is read against that scale. Against it the design does not
identify the graph-evidence effect: score summaries were recorded under
unequal measurement policies, the archived localization diagnostics are
inconclusive, and the separately protocolled bundle trial returned a
negative point estimate with no detected improvement. One post-hoc,
mechanism-consistent event pairs a large starvation-exit reduction with
score movement; its processor left the configuration four rounds later,
and without a same-window counterfactual it does not isolate that
processor's efficacy.

We state the consequence as a proposition --- self-evolution is variation
plus selection plus \emph{readout}, and on this bed and system class
score-based retention may mis-rank effects small relative to readout
variation. Non-positive cumulative gain is a possible dynamic, not one
estimated here. We also offer a four-rung evidence ladder: R0 to R2 are
live in GHX, R3 (did the edit change outcomes) only as a hand-run
efficacy-trial protocol.
\end{abstract}

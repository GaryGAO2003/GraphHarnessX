\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{ch:related}
% =========================================================================

\claim{Four literature routes independently leave one position unoccupied
--- provenance-grounded self-evolution --- and all four select on aggregate
score, the signal this thesis demonstrates deaf on noisy beds.}

The four routes are \S\ref{sec:self-evolving}--\S\ref{sec:structured-spaces}.
\S\ref{sec:graph-memory} covers an adjacent line on graph memory, included
only to mark where the boundary of this work runs, not as a fifth route.
\S\ref{sec:positioning} states the position, names the systems that come
closest, and concedes what each of them already holds.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Self-evolving agent harnesses}
\label{sec:self-evolving}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The coordinates for this line are set by a recent survey~\cite{survey-selfevolve},
which organizes the field along what, when, how and where an agent evolves.
On its \emph{where} axis the object of this thesis is harness
configuration: not model weights, and not memory alone.

\paragraph{Free-code candidate spaces.} ADAS~\cite{adas} and the G\"odel
Agent~\cite{godel-agent} let an agent rewrite its own code directly. The
Darwin G\"odel Machine~\cite{dgm} adds a population archive of
self-modifying agents --- the nearest precedent for the layered hash
identity of \S\ref{sec:identity}. The Huxley--G\"odel
Machine~\cite{hgm} changes \emph{whose} score is aggregated, not the type
of the signal. SICA~\cite{sica} closes a self-improvement loop on coding
agents.

\paragraph{Workflow evolution.} AFlow~\cite{aflow} and its successor
A$^2$Flow~\cite{a2flow} evolve agentic workflows, with candidate
representations converging over time toward typed graphs.
GEPA~\cite{gepa} performs reflective prompt evolution.
AlphaEvolve~\cite{alphaevolve} contributes a cascade of evaluation gates
--- reusable verification infrastructure, though what each gate verifies is
that the score improved. EvoAgentX~\cite{evoagentx} integrates several of
these into a platform.

\paragraph{The closest problem setting.} HarnessForge~\cite{harnessforge}
targets the same object as this thesis: it treats the harness as a
(Planning, Action, Memory) text triple and evolves it by
fault-attribution-guided archive search with a trained policy, validating
candidates through an interface smoke test and a Pareto fitness. Same
problem, opposite route. It has no typed build-time validation, no
graph-hash identity, and no reconciliation between what a candidate
declares and what execution observed --- and it trains, where GHX is
training-free and structural. It is named here as the nearest neighbour
rather than left for a reader to discover.

\paragraph{The harness-level line in 2026.} The object of this thesis
acquired a literature of its own during the year it was written, and the
positions worth naming are these. \textbf{NLAH}~\cite{nlah} lifts the
harness out of controller code into an editable natural-language document
interpreted at runtime, cutting harness tokens by $95\%$ and beating a
code harness on Live-SWE; its own appendix concedes that natural-language
imprecision is ``under-specified, interpreted differently across models,
or weakened by paraphrase'' --- there is no machine-checkable constraint.
It is the representational rival of this thesis: same pain, opposite
remedy, and \S\ref{sec:positioning} takes the conflict head-on.
\textbf{SIGIL}~\cite{sigil} goes the other way, compiling prose skills
into a typed harness behind a compile gate that refuses any rule without
a source citation --- the nearest precedent for a build-time gate, on a
single skill rather than a whole harness. \textbf{HarnessBank}~\cite{harnessbank}
keeps a gene bank of harnesses and screens mutations before expensive
evaluation with a significance-gated criterion
($\hat{\Delta} > 0$ and $z \ge 1.96$), reporting that the gate eliminates
the \emph{false elites} an ungated bank admits; it is the one system in
this survey whose admission is a statistical test rather than a raw
score, and it rejects non-regression criteria by name --- though on
Terminal-Bench and SWE-bench, and without treating truncation.
\textbf{AgentDevel}~\cite{agentdevel} reframes self-evolution as release
engineering: versioned specifications, symptom-level diagnosis, and
regression-aware gated promotion. And three works circle the question this
thesis ends on. \emph{Do Agent Optimizers Compound?}~\cite{compound} asks
whether multi-round optimizer gains accumulate across rounds --- the
treadmill of \S\ref{sec:archaeology}, posed as a benchmark question. The
\emph{Red Queen G\"odel Machine}~\cite{redqueen} co-evolves the evaluator
with the evaluated to stop a loop over-fitting a static judge.
\textbf{EMAS}~\cite{emas} turns traces into structured diagnoses and
refuses to land a revision until its diagnosis has reproduced across
samples and passed validation, and \textbf{WLML}~\cite{wlml} attributes
failures to a workflow node and its mechanism before repairing it ---
both are steps toward an admission that reads evidence rather than
score, and neither reads the executed graph.

\paragraph{Generation against admission.} One distinction runs through this
entire section and is easy to lose. GEPA \emph{reads execution traces} ---
but only to \emph{generate} candidates; admission remains Pareto on scores.
Several systems use trace text to propose. \textbf{None admits a candidate
on the grounds that its mechanism actually fired in execution.} Selection
is aggregate score or Pareto in every system surveyed here, HarnessForge
included, whose attribution guides generation while admission stays
Pareto-on-fitness; HarnessBank is the single exception, and what it admits
on is a significance test over the same score.

\paragraph{Evaluation and readability, orthogonal concerns.} A
contemporaneous critique~\cite{rethink-harness-eval} argues that
harness-evolution methods which search and then report on the same
public benchmark conflate two questions the field has not separated, a
concern this thesis's own evidence argument echoes inside one system
rather than across a leaderboard. \textbf{Harness
Handbook}~\cite{harness-handbook} makes an evolving harness's own
artifacts readable, navigable and editable by a human, a different axis
this thesis does not attempt.

\section{Failure attribution: from flat logs to causal graphs}
\label{sec:attribution}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{Flat text fails on its own numbers}

The first wave of failure attribution had an LLM read flat logs.
MAST~\cite{mast} builds a taxonomy of multi-agent failure modes.
Who\&When~\cite{whowhen} reports \textbf{14.2\%} step-level accuracy.
AgenTracer~\cite{agentracer} and RAFFLES~\cite{raffles} refine the
approach. TRAIL~\cite{trail} reports its best model at roughly
\textbf{11\%} localization on its own 148-trace benchmark.

These are the field's own numbers on the field's own benchmarks, and they
are the reason the route moved.

\subsection*{The field's own answer is structure}

The second wave replaces flat text with causal graphs. CHIEF~\cite{chief}
makes the move explicit in its title. FALAT~\cite{falat} follows.
GRADE~\cite{grade} grades edges as observed, declared or inferred --- the
direct ancestor of the declaration-against-observation reconciliation of
\S\ref{sec:unfolded}, and evidence that reconstruction error is a
recognized cost in this line.

This thesis is not alone in demanding structure. The route converged on it
independently.

\subsection*{The nearest counter-examples, named}

Four works do connect attribution back to a repair action, and the claim
that attribution is ``offline, after the run'' holds for the first wave but
not for them:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AgentDebug}~\cite{agentdebug}: detect, attribute, recover,
        re-run.
  \item \textbf{DoVer}~\cite{dover}: intervene at the suspected step,
        re-run, and count single-intervention flips.
  \item \textbf{CausalFlow}~\cite{causalflow}: step-level counterfactual
        responsibility, feeding repair and training pairs.
  \item \textbf{FlowFixer}~\cite{flowfixer}: symbolic root cause to
        pre-execution filter to validated repair, judge-free, reporting
        $71.3\%$ repair success across three platforms.
\end{itemize}

Causal Agent Replay~\cite{car} is deliberately \emph{not} in this set: per
our own reading it stops at diagnosis and was not reproduced in a live
system. It enters this thesis only as a transplant donor
(\S\ref{sec:positioning}).

\subsection*{The gap, at the precision the counter-examples force}

Against those four, the position has to be stated in four parts rather than
one:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Native recording.} $U$ is written by the harness runtime;
        every edge is observed-grade and reconstruction error is zero.
        GRADE's three-tier edge grading is that pain made explicit. And the
        object recorded is the \emph{dynamic unfolded execution}, where the
        closest neighbours reason over a static orchestration graph.
  \item \textbf{Cross-task population aggregation.} Lift, cone diffs and
        regression accounts are computed over a whole bed. Three of the
        four counter-examples are single-trace diagnoses; the exception,
        FlowFixer, aggregates across platforms rather than across a bed
        inside one evolving system.
  \item \textbf{Read--write closure.} The evidence feeds the Evolver,
        passes gates, and lands in the configuration through a
        transactional apply, with the genotype graph paired to $U$. Their
        graphs land back in nothing that persists in the harness.
  \item \textbf{Resident, cross-round intervention.} The edit stays
        installed and is re-judged every round, rather than repairing one
        trajectory once. This is testable: an intervention is resident if
        it survives into round $k{+}1$'s configuration.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Where the advantage is largest, and where it is narrow.} Of the
four, DoVer is the nearest in spirit --- it intervenes and re-runs, which
is the shape of an efficacy readout --- and it is where the distance is
greatest on every one of the four parts: its graph is reconstructed after
the fact from a single trace, its intervention is a one-off re-run of that
trace, nothing it learns persists into a harness, and nothing is
re-judged next round. GHX records the edges as they happen, aggregates
over the bed, writes the edit into the configuration, and re-judges it
every round. Against FlowFixer the distance is narrower and is stated as
such: it is symbolic and judge-free, which GHX's gates also are, and its
$71.3\%$ repair success is a number this thesis does not have an
equivalent for. What FlowFixer does not have is residence: its repair is
validated once, on one platform's trace, and does not survive into a
loop that would re-judge it.

A 2026 survey~\cite{survey-attr-evolve} states directly that attribution
and self-evolution are unconnected lines. That sentence is the one this
chapter is built around, and the join below is what it leaves undone.

\paragraph{And the join this thesis supplies.} The literature measures
attribution accuracy \emph{offline, on benchmarks built for it}, and
reports 14.2\% to roughly 11\%. Chapter~\ref{ch:baseline} measures
something the field has not: what happens when a signal of that quality is
wired into a \emph{live retention loop} --- consumed by the Planner, quoted
verbatim in candidate documents, and shipped on. The field has quantified
the instrument. The consequence of deploying it has not been quantified
before.

\section{Execution provenance and AgentOps}
\label{sec:provenance}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

A parallel line treats execution records as first-class infrastructure:
OpenTelemetry's \texttt{gen\_ai} semantic conventions, AgentOps
taxonomies, eBPF-based observability.

A 2026 survey of this line~\cite{survey-provenance} defines execution
provenance as a typed execution graph over six edge classes --- and finds
that it serves \emph{human} trust, audit and debugging only. Attributed to
that survey as of its cutoff, rather than asserted here as a universal
claim: \textbf{no published work closes provenance into an automated
improvement loop.}

That sentence is the direct motivation for GHX, and it is the one place in
this chapter where the gap is stated by someone other than us.

\section{Structured candidate spaces}
\label{sec:structured-spaces}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two systems in this line are donors to GHX rather than neighbours, and
each is analysed here to the level of what was taken and what was
changed, because the honest statement of the contribution depends on
that level.

\paragraph{MermaidFlow: what was taken, what was changed.}
MermaidFlow~\cite{mermaidflow} represents a workflow as a Mermaid graph
over six typed declarative units, with edges carrying type-constrained
execution flow and data labels, and evolves it with graph-edit operators
--- crossover, mutation, insert, delete --- under a validator. The
validator is syntactic: five soft checks implemented as regular
expressions (interface exists, problem-to-return connectivity, type
membership, type legality, ensemble in-degree) plus a Mermaid compile.
That is enough to lift the valid-candidate rate above $90\%$ against
roughly $50\%$ for AFlow, at a mean score of $80.75$ against $78.67$.
\emph{Taken:} the premise that the candidate space should be graph edits
under a validator rather than free text, and the operator vocabulary ---
GHX's seven typed operations (\S\ref{sec:candidate-surface}) are that
vocabulary specialized to a processor plane. \emph{Changed:} three
things. The object is the harness's composition layer, not a per-task
workflow. The validator is semantic and build-time rather than syntactic:
a write\,$\to$\,reload\,$\to$\,re-graph round trip, a parity door against
the legacy runloop, and a hash-contract test. And the identity problem is
taken seriously: MermaidFlow's closure lemma states that validated edits
compose ``without revalidation'', yet per our mechanism-level read the
implementation re-runs the whole checker on every candidate --- no
incremental revalidation, no graph-hash identity, and no reconciliation
of what a candidate declares against what execution observed, because
there is no execution trace in the system at all. Those three are what
GHX adds, and the head-on conflict is stated: the ``free of
revalidation'' design does not hold at the composition layer, where an
edit to one processor's ordering changes what every later processor
sees.

\paragraph{AgentFlow: what was taken, what was changed.}
AgentFlow's agent dependency graph~\cite{agentflow} recovers, from
heterogeneous agent frameworks, six typed node classes (agent, prompt,
model, capability, memory, policy) and \textbf{three strongly-typed edge
families}: component dependency, guarded control flow, and data flow
including state reads and writes. It is static and LLM-free, and it is
an over-approximation by design --- ``possible dependencies rather than a
single concrete execution trace'' --- recovering a median of 17 nodes
and 28 edges on 59 of 60 frameworks and feeding a bill of materials and a
taint analysis. \emph{Taken:} the three edge families, verbatim
(\S\ref{sec:composition-graph}), and the reaching-definitions treatment
of state reads and writes that gives GHX's data edges their semantics
(\S\ref{sec:unfolded}). \emph{Changed:} the direction of every one of
its design choices. AgentFlow is offline, static and possible; the
unfolded graph $U$ is online, concrete and observed --- written by the
runtime for one invocation, with an edge present only if the dependency
it names actually occurred. AgentFlow's product is a report read by a
person; $U$ is read by an acceptance gate. And its node vocabulary is
deliberately not adopted, because it describes a design space, whereas
GHX's has to describe one running harness, so the node types are the
harness's own: hook points, processors, slots, bundles, skills, tools.
The relation is the one this chapter uses for the attribution line too:
AgentFlow is the static over-approximation; GHX is its dynamic,
single-trace counterpart.

Modular design-space systems --- AgentSquare~\cite{agentsquare} and
EvoFlow~\cite{evoflow} --- type candidates at module level and search a
modular design space; MaAS~\cite{maas} frames the same move as sampling
an agentic supernet. Admission there is still a benchmark score, and none
of them reads the executed trace.

\paragraph{What they enhance, and what none does.} This line buys
\emph{legality} of the candidate space: typed units, constraint-preserving
edits, high valid-code rates. What none of them does is connect the typed
candidate to evidence that it \emph{fired}. Typing governs what may be
\emph{proposed}; it never governs what \emph{did} happen.

\section{Graph memory and experience reuse}
\label{sec:graph-memory}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

This line is adjacent rather than on the route, and is included to mark the
boundary.

MEGA~\cite{mega} is the only neighbour satisfying all three of the criteria
fixed before scoring --- an evidence graph, refinement over it, and
cross-round persistence. Its graph, however, is LLM-distilled
\emph{knowledge} and is not interventionable; GHX's is execution-level and
supports do-style queries. \emph{The differentiation is in what can be
asked of the graph, not in a measured downstream gain}, which is consistent
with this thesis's own claim of legality and identity rather than reach.

GraphMind~\cite{graphmind} reuses winning subgraphs, and feeds one of GHX's
guidance channels. EXG~\cite{exg} organizes execution history as an
experience graph --- but the object is retrieval memory, not an executable
task graph, and reuse is retrieval rather than admission.

\paragraph{Boundary.} This line evolves what the agent \emph{remembers};
GHX evolves what the harness \emph{is}. The distinction is worth holding,
because \S\ref{sec:survivor} reports that the one channel which produced a
positive result in this study fell on the memory side of it.

\section{Positioning}
\label{sec:positioning}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The position is the conjunction: execution provenance (record) $\times$
automated attribution (diagnose) $\times$ typed graph edits written back
into a persisted harness (edit), closed into one loop. \textbf{The claim is
that no prior system holds all of these at once --- not that any one of
them is new.}

\subsection*{Head-on conflicts, stated}

Four conflicts are taken directly rather than routed around: free-code
candidate spaces~\cite{adas,dgm}, where this thesis argues the candidate
should be typed; natural-language harnesses~\cite{nlah}, where it argues
that a harness whose constraints cannot be machine-checked cannot be
gated, and whose own appendix says so; knowledge-level
graphs~\cite{mega}, where it argues the graph should be execution-level;
and score-only gate ladders~\cite{alphaevolve,harnessbank}, where it
argues the gate should read the execution rather than the score ---
HarnessBank's significance gate being the strongest form of the position
it argues against, since a significance test over a score that is not a
monotone transducer of mechanism (\S\ref{sec:gain-face}) is a rigorous
test of the wrong quantity.

\subsection*{Transplant registry}

Eleven mechanisms are borrowed from outside this thesis's own line, each
named with its donor, so that no borrowed idea is presented as invented
here: an in-run redundancy guard~\cite{ledger-t17}; a groundless-answer
gate~\cite{mapgraph}; model-node intent annotation~\cite{aer};
winning-subgraph feedback~\cite{graphmind}; typed evidence
atoms~\cite{mega}; symptom-to-bucket mapping~\cite{harnessfix}; anchored
fork replay~\cite{shepherd,car}; an edge-level do
operator~\cite{e2explainer}; a held-out final gate~\cite{regimes}; a
cross-model transfer check~\cite{better-harnesses}; and free sibling
controls~\cite{craft}. Anytime-valid sequential testing~\cite{pace} is
cited beside the registry as the precedent for the envelope gate of
\S\ref{sec:power}.

\subsection*{Caveats on this survey}

Preprints from 2026 are unreviewed. This is a three-way scan across the
routes above, not a systematic review. The reinforcement-learning side of
self-improvement was scanned only at the margin, and any claim of
unoccupancy is scoped accordingly.

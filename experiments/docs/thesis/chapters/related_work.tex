\claim{Four literature routes independently leave one position unoccupied
--- provenance-grounded self-evolution --- and all four select on aggregate
score, the signal this thesis demonstrates deaf on noisy beds.}

The four routes are \S\ref{sec:self-evolving}--\S\ref{sec:structured-spaces}.
\S\ref{sec:graph-memory} covers an adjacent line on graph memory, included

\section{Self-evolving agent harnesses}
\label{sec:self-evolving}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The coordinates for this line are set by a recent survey~\cite{survey-selfevolve},
which organizes the field along what, when, how and where an agent evolves.
On its \emph{where} axis the object of this thesis is harness
configuration: not model weights, and not memory alone.

\paragraph{What the candidate is.} Two answers dominate. Free code:
ADAS~\cite{adas} lets an agent rewrite itself, and the Darwin G\"odel
Machine~\cite{dgm} adds a population archive of self-modifying agents ---
the nearest precedent for the layered hash identity of
\S\ref{sec:identity}. Or a workflow: AFlow~\cite{aflow} evolves agentic
workflows with candidate representations converging over time toward typed
graphs, and GEPA~\cite{gepa} performs reflective prompt evolution.
AlphaEvolve~\cite{alphaevolve} contributes a cascade of evaluation gates
--- reusable verification infrastructure, though what each gate verifies is
that the score improved.

\paragraph{The closest problem setting.} HarnessForge~\cite{harnessforge}
targets the same object as this thesis: it treats the harness as a
(Planning, Action, Memory) text triple and evolves it by
fault-attribution-guided archive search with a trained policy, validating
candidates through an interface smoke test and a Pareto fitness. Same
problem, opposite route. It has no typed build-time validation, no
graph-hash identity, and no reconciliation between what a candidate
declares and what execution observed --- and it trains, where GHX is
training-free and structural.

\paragraph{The harness-level line in 2026.} The object of this thesis
acquired a literature of its own during the year it was written, and the
positions worth naming are these. \textbf{NLAH}~\cite{nlah} lifts the
harness out of controller code into an editable natural-language document
interpreted at runtime, cutting harness tokens by $95\%$ and beating a
code harness on Live-SWE; its own appendix concedes that natural-language
imprecision is ``under-specified, interpreted differently across models,
or weakened by paraphrase'' --- there is no machine-checkable constraint.
It is the representational rival of this thesis --- same problem, opposite
remedy --- and the conflict is taken up in \S\ref{sec:positioning}.
\textbf{SIGIL}~\cite{sigil} goes the other way, compiling prose skills
into a typed harness behind a compile gate that refuses any rule without
a source citation --- the nearest precedent for a build-time gate, on a
single skill rather than a whole harness. \textbf{HarnessBank}~\cite{harnessbank}
keeps a gene bank of harnesses and screens mutations before expensive
evaluation with a paired significance gate --- $\hat{\Delta} > 0$ and
$z \ge 1.96$, the critical value of a two-sided $5\%$ test applied in the
positive direction only --- reporting that it eliminates the \emph{false
elites}, gains indistinguishable from noise, that an ungated bank admits.
It is the one system in this survey whose admission is a statistical test
rather than a raw score, and it names non-regression criteria among the
screening rules it argues are too weak. Its seven domains include
SWE-bench, where its own report concedes that $n{=}26$ cannot separate the
arms; truncation is not treated.
\textbf{AgentDevel}~\cite{agentdevel} reframes self-evolution as release
engineering: versioned specifications, symptom-level diagnosis, and
regression-aware gated promotion. Two works reach further in the direction
this thesis takes. \textbf{EMAS}~\cite{emas} refuses to land a revision
until its diagnosis has reproduced across samples --- an admission that
reads evidence rather than score, though not the executed graph. And
\emph{Do Agent Optimizers Compound?}~\cite{compound} asks whether
multi-round gains accumulate at all, which is the treadmill of
\S\ref{sec:archaeology} posed as a benchmark question.

\paragraph{Generation against admission.} Several systems read trace text
to \emph{propose} --- GEPA reads execution traces, HarnessForge is guided
by attribution --- and then admit on Pareto-on-score anyway.
\textbf{None admits a candidate on the grounds that its mechanism actually
fired in execution.} HarnessBank is the single exception to score-only
admission, and what it tests is significance over the same score.

\paragraph{Evaluation, an orthogonal concern.} A contemporaneous
critique~\cite{rethink-harness-eval} argues that harness-evolution methods
which search and then report on the same public benchmark conflate two
questions the field has not separated --- a concern this thesis's own
evidence argument echoes inside one system rather than across a
leaderboard. None of the harness-level works surveyed
here reports an independent execution of another group's released loop,
and to our knowledge the AEGIS release had not been re-executed outside
its authors' group before Chapter~\ref{ch:baseline}.

\section{Failure attribution: from flat logs to causal graphs}
\label{sec:attribution}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{Flat text fails on its own numbers}

The first wave had an LLM read flat logs, and reported its own ceiling:
Who\&When~\cite{whowhen} \textbf{14.2\%} step-level accuracy,
TRAIL~\cite{trail} roughly \textbf{11\%} localization for its best model
on its own 148-trace benchmark. Those are the field's numbers on the
field's benchmarks, and the reason the route moved to structure.

\subsection*{The field's own answer is structure}

The second wave replaces flat text with causal graphs: CHIEF~\cite{chief}
makes the move explicit in its title, and GRADE~\cite{grade} grades edges
as observed, declared or inferred --- the direct ancestor of the
declaration-against-observation reconciliation of \S\ref{sec:unfolded},
and evidence that reconstruction error is a recognized cost in this line.
The route converged on structure independently of this thesis.

\subsection*{The nearest counter-examples, named}

Four works do connect attribution back to a repair action, and the claim
that attribution is ``offline, after the run'' holds for the first wave but
not for them:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AgentDebug}~\cite{agentdebug}: detect, attribute, recover,
        re-run.
  \item \textbf{DoVer}~\cite{dover}: intervene at the suspected step,
        re-run, and count single-intervention flips.
  \item \textbf{CausalFlow}~\cite{causalflow}: step-level counterfactual
        responsibility, feeding repair and training pairs.
  \item \textbf{FlowFixer}~\cite{flowfixer}: symbolic root cause to
        pre-execution filter to validated repair, judge-free, reporting
        $71.3\%$ repair success across three platforms.
\end{itemize}

Causal Agent Replay~\cite{car} is deliberately \emph{not} in this set: per
our own reading it stops at diagnosis and was not reproduced in a live
system. It enters this thesis only as a transplant donor
(\S\ref{sec:positioning}).

\subsection*{The gap, in four parts}

Against those four the position separates into four parts:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Native recording.} $U$ is written by the runtime, so every
        edge is observed-grade and reconstruction error is zero --- the
        pain GRADE's three-tier grading makes explicit --- and the object
        recorded is the dynamic unfolded execution, where the neighbours
        reason over a static orchestration graph.
  \item \textbf{Cross-task aggregation.} Lift, cone diffs and regression
        accounts are computed over a whole bed. Three of the four are
        single-trace diagnoses; FlowFixer aggregates across platforms
        rather than across a bed inside one evolving system.
  \item \textbf{Read--write closure.} The evidence feeds the Evolver,
        passes gates, and lands in the configuration through a
        transactional apply, genotype paired to $U$. Their graphs land
        back in nothing that persists.
  \item \textbf{Resident intervention.} The edit stays installed and is
        re-judged every round rather than repairing one trajectory once,
        which is testable: it is resident if it survives into round
        $k{+}1$'s configuration.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Where the advantage is largest, and where it is narrow.} DoVer
is nearest in spirit --- it intervenes and re-runs, the shape of an
efficacy readout --- and furthest on all four parts: a graph reconstructed
after the fact from one trace, a one-off intervention on that trace,
nothing persisted into a harness, nothing re-judged next round. Against
FlowFixer the distance is narrower: it is symbolic and judge-free, which
GHX's gates also are, and its $71.3\%$ repair success is a number this
thesis has no equivalent for. What it lacks is residence --- its repair is
validated once and does not survive into a loop that would re-judge it.

A 2026 survey~\cite{survey-attr-evolve} states directly that attribution
and self-evolution are unconnected lines.

\paragraph{The join.} The literature measures
attribution accuracy \emph{offline, on benchmarks built for it}, and
reports 14.2\% to roughly 11\%. Chapter~\ref{ch:baseline} measures
something the field has not: what happens when a signal of that quality is
wired into a \emph{live retention loop} --- consumed by the Planner, quoted
verbatim in candidate documents, and shipped on. The field has quantified
the instrument; the consequence of deploying it has not.

\section{Execution provenance and AgentOps}
\label{sec:provenance}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

A parallel line treats execution records as first-class infrastructure:
OpenTelemetry's \texttt{gen\_ai} semantic conventions, AgentOps
taxonomies, eBPF-based observability.

A 2026 survey of this line~\cite{survey-provenance} defines execution
provenance as a typed execution graph over six edge classes --- and finds
that it serves \emph{human} trust, audit and debugging only. Attributed to
that survey as of its cutoff, rather than asserted here as a universal
claim: \textbf{no published work closes provenance into an automated
improvement loop.}

That finding is the direct motivation for GHX.

\section{Structured candidate spaces}
\label{sec:structured-spaces}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two systems in this line are donors to GHX rather than neighbours; each is
analysed by what was taken and what was changed.

\paragraph{MermaidFlow: what was taken, what was changed.}
MermaidFlow~\cite{mermaidflow} represents a workflow as a Mermaid graph
over six typed declarative units and evolves it with graph-edit operators
--- crossover, mutation, insert, delete --- under a validator of five
regular-expression checks plus a Mermaid compile. That is enough to lift
the valid-candidate rate above $90\%$ against roughly $50\%$ for AFlow, at
a mean score of $80.75$ against $78.67$. \emph{Taken:} the premise that
the candidate space should be graph edits under a validator rather than
free text, and the operator vocabulary --- GHX's seven typed operations
(\S\ref{sec:candidate-surface}) are that vocabulary specialized to a
processor plane. \emph{Changed:} the object is the harness's composition
layer, not a per-task workflow; the validator is semantic and build-time
rather than syntactic (a write\,$\to$\,reload\,$\to$\,re-graph round trip,
a parity door, a hash-contract test); and identity is treated as a
problem. MermaidFlow's closure lemma states that validated edits compose
``without revalidation'', yet per our mechanism-level read the
implementation re-runs the whole checker on every candidate --- no
incremental revalidation, no graph-hash identity, and no reconciliation of
declaration against observation, there being no execution trace in the
system at all. The ``free of revalidation'' design does not hold at the
composition layer, where an edit to one processor's ordering changes what
every later processor sees.

\paragraph{AgentFlow: what was taken, what was changed.}
AgentFlow's agent dependency graph~\cite{agentflow} recovers, from
heterogeneous agent frameworks, six typed node classes (agent, prompt,
model, capability, memory, policy) and \textbf{three strongly-typed edge
families}: component dependency, guarded control flow, and data flow
including state reads and writes. It is static, LLM-free and an
over-approximation by design --- ``possible dependencies rather than a
single concrete execution trace'' --- recovering a median of 17 nodes and
28 edges on 59 of 60 frameworks. \emph{Taken:} the three edge families,
verbatim (\S\ref{sec:composition-graph}), and the reaching-definitions
treatment of state reads and writes that gives GHX's data edges their
semantics (\S\ref{sec:unfolded}). \emph{Changed:} the direction of every
one of its design choices. AgentFlow is offline, static and possible;
$U$ is online, concrete and observed, an edge present only if the
dependency it names occurred. AgentFlow's product is a report read by a
person; $U$ is read by an acceptance gate. Its node vocabulary is not
adopted, because it describes a design space where GHX's must describe one
running harness --- hook points, processors, slots, bundles, skills,
tools. AgentFlow is the static over-approximation; GHX its dynamic,
single-trace counterpart.

\paragraph{What they enhance, and what none does.} This line buys
\emph{legality} of the candidate space: typed units, constraint-preserving
edits, high valid-code rates. What none of them does is connect the typed
candidate to evidence that it \emph{fired}. Typing governs what may be
\emph{proposed}; it never governs what \emph{did} happen.

\section{Graph memory and experience reuse}
\label{sec:graph-memory}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

This line is adjacent rather than on the route, and is included to mark the
boundary.

MEGA~\cite{mega} is the only neighbour satisfying all three of the criteria
fixed before scoring --- an evidence graph, refinement over it, and
cross-round persistence. Its graph, however, is LLM-distilled
\emph{knowledge} and is not interventionable; GHX's is execution-level and
supports do-style queries. The differentiation is in what can be asked of the graph, not in a
measured downstream gain.

GraphMind~\cite{graphmind} reuses winning subgraphs, and feeds one of
GHX's guidance channels. Where this line organizes execution history for
retrieval, GHX records it for admission.

\paragraph{Boundary.} This line evolves what the agent \emph{remembers};
GHX evolves what the harness \emph{is}. \S\ref{sec:survivor} reports that
the one channel which produced a positive result in this study fell on the
memory side of that distinction.

\subsection*{Head-on conflicts}

Four conflicts are taken directly: free-code
candidate spaces~\cite{adas,dgm}, where this thesis argues the candidate
should be typed; natural-language harnesses~\cite{nlah}, where it argues
that a harness whose constraints cannot be machine-checked cannot be
gated, and whose own appendix says so; knowledge-level
graphs~\cite{mega}, where it argues the graph should be execution-level;
and score-only gate ladders~\cite{alphaevolve,harnessbank}, where it
argues the gate should read the execution rather than the score ---
HarnessBank's significance gate being the strongest form of the position
it argues against, since a significance test over a score that is not a
monotone transducer of mechanism (\S\ref{sec:gain-face}) is a rigorous
test of the wrong quantity.

\subsection*{Transplant registry}

Eleven mechanisms are borrowed from outside this thesis's own line, each
named with its donor: an in-run redundancy guard~\cite{ledger-t17}; a groundless-answer
gate~\cite{mapgraph}; model-node intent annotation~\cite{aer};
winning-subgraph feedback~\cite{graphmind}; typed evidence
atoms~\cite{mega}; symptom-to-bucket mapping~\cite{harnessfix}; anchored
fork replay~\cite{shepherd,car}; an edge-level do
operator~\cite{e2explainer}; a held-out final gate~\cite{regimes}; a
cross-model transfer check~\cite{better-harnesses}; and free sibling
controls~\cite{craft}. Anytime-valid sequential testing~\cite{pace} is
cited beside the registry as the precedent for the envelope gate of
\S\ref{sec:power}.

\subsection*{Caveats on this survey}

Preprints from 2026 are unreviewed. This is a three-way scan across the
routes above, not a systematic review. The reinforcement-learning side of
self-improvement was scanned only at the margin, and any claim of
unoccupancy is scoped accordingly.

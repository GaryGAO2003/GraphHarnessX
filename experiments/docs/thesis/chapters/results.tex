\section{Scores and measurement design}

\subsection{Beds, arms, and the switch ladder}
\label{sec:beds}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The bed is the \textbf{100-task no-pixel subset} of GAIA's text-only
split, for the four reasons \S\ref{sec:what-was-run} gives.


The \emph{no-graph} arm is the official system with every GHX flag off.
The \emph{graph} arm is the same system with the \textbf{whole stack on}
--- recording, graph evidence, graph verification and the typed candidate
surface together. The switches are cumulative by construction, each level
keeping the ones below, but no campaign was flown at an intermediate
level, which is why \S\ref{sec:localization} reports no component
ablation: there are no per-level arms to attribute an effect to.

Earlier graph campaigns
flown before the graph-layer fixes are excluded by a scope ruling
(\S\ref{sec:scope}, Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Scoring is audited
throughout.

One bookkeeping point governs every cross-arm number. The arms did not fly
quite the same bed: GAIA's text-only split holds 103 tasks, three of them
carrying pixel content the recorder could not attribute, and while the
graph arms always flew the 100 without them, the no-graph arm flew all 103
on the first two seeds and switched to the 100 at round 7 of the third.
The loop's own \texttt{curves.json} records whichever bed each round
actually flew and is therefore not comparable across arms, so every
cross-arm reading in this thesis is recomputed from \texttt{task\_history}
on the 100-task subset common to all six campaigns \F{46}. Three of those
100 are never solved in 9{,}600 evaluations and contribute no variance, so
every reported \emph{difference} is identical on 97 or on 100 \F{54}; the
100-task denominator is kept regardless, since dropping tasks on the basis
of their outcomes is selection.

\subsubsection*{Models}

Every campaign runs the same two models through a LiteLLM gateway, and the
runbooks record the pair on every launch line: the \textbf{solver} is
\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} (284B parameters, 13B active; thinking enabled,
effort high), and the \textbf{four meta-roles and the judge} are
\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} (1.6T, 49B active). The official paper's one
complete experiment has the same shape --- a stronger meta tier over a
weaker task tier, Claude Opus over Sonnet, GPT and Qwen --- on a different
benchmark, so the comparison across the two is one of form, not of
number.

The split follows the published model cards, fixed before any campaign
flew.\footnote{DeepSeek-V4 model cards, Hugging Face, accessed 4 September
2026 (\url{https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash}), and the
API pricing page (\url{https://deepseek.ai/pricing}): BrowseComp Pass@1
V4-Pro Max $83.4$, V4-Flash High $53.5$; MRCR-1M V4-Flash Non-Think
$37.5$; list price per million tokens \$0.14 / \$0.28 against \$0.435 /
\$0.87.} Pro on the solver would have flattened the window a harness
effect has to show in --- it scores $83.4$ on BrowseComp, the published
benchmark closest to this bed, near the open-model ceiling --- where Flash
sits mid-curve at $53.5$ and costs a third as much, which is what makes
the three-seed replication of \S\ref{sec:replication} affordable at all.
Flash's one documented hard limit, long-context collapse with thinking
off, is avoided: thinking is on in every trajectory here.  Claims are scoped to this pair; no claim in this thesis
concerns model tiers.

\subsection{Power arithmetic first}
\label{sec:power}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{The flip rate}

Take two consecutive rounds in which nothing shipped. The configuration is
identical; the only difference is that the bed was run again. Across the
six such windows in the baseline campaign's sixteen-round readout,
\textbf{21.0\%} of task outcomes flip --- $126$ of $600$ task-pairs, 95\%
Wilson interval $[17.9\%, 24.4\%]$ \F{15}. The other two seeds give
$20.1\%$ and $19.2\%$, and pooled over all twenty windows the figure is
\textbf{20.1\%} on $402/2{,}000$ pairs, $[18.4\%, 21.9\%]$, with the three
per-seed intervals overlapping heavily \F{9e}. A task-clustered bootstrap
--- resampling the 100 tasks rather than the pairs, since flip propensity
is a property of the task --- widens this to $[16.5\%, 23.9\%]$ \F{57}. The noise floor is a property of the bed, not
of a run.

Over those windows, $52.3\%$ of task-campaign pairs flip at least once.
The resulting total-score swing for a single window runs from $-8$ to
$+5$ tasks \F{15}\F{44}\F{45}.

\subsubsection*{The arithmetic}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item One fifth of task outcomes flip on re-measurement with no treatment
        applied.
  \item The bed therefore swings by up to eight tasks between two
        identical rounds.
\end{enumerate}



\subsubsection*{One envelope, one caliber}

The swing is quoted throughout as an \emph{observed range} over ship-free
windows, and the range depends on how many windows were drawn:
$-4\ldots{+}5$ on seed 1 (6 windows), $-4\ldots{+}2$ on seed 2 (8
windows), and $-8\ldots{+}5$ on seed 3 (6 windows), all on the
config-identity window rule this thesis quotes throughout
\F{15}\F{9e}; F44 and F45 record what a second labelling rule gives for
the same two seeds.
The band used for every readability judgment in this thesis is the union,
$\mathbf{-8\ldots{+}5}$ tasks, quoted that way everywhere; per-seed
ranges appear only where a seed is being described.

A range over a handful of windows is not a standard deviation. Across the
twenty adjacent same-configuration windows, the sample SD of paired score
changes is $\mathbf{3.70}$ tasks; three times that descriptive scale is
$11.1$ \F{15}. Neither quantity is a formal detection threshold or a
per-round score SD. The one number on
which the two calibers disagree is the $+9$ of \S\ref{sec:one-repair},
discussed there and in \S\ref{sec:readout}.

\subsubsection*{There is no informative-and-reliable subset of this bed}

The natural response to a noisy bed is to find its reliable core and
measure there. The census forecloses it. Across the six whitelist campaigns on the 100-task subset ---
9{,}600 task evaluations --- \textbf{3} tasks are never solved,
\textbf{18} always pass, and \textbf{79 are volatile} \F{54}.

The 21 stable tasks contribute no variance and can register no treatment
effect; the 79 that \emph{can} move are the ones that move unaided.
Removing the volatile family removes the instrument rather than cleaning
it.

Bed reliability here cannot be bought by item selection. It can be bought
in repetitions --- a Spearman--Brown calculation prices that against the
measured per-task flip rate --- or by changing the readout, which is this
thesis's own route (\S\ref{sec:future}). Priced: a single draw's
reliability is $0.57$, and \textbf{four} averaged repeats are the first
integer count reaching this thesis's own $0.80$ ``reliable'' bar
(F16/F54) \F{60}.

\subsubsection*{Operational deviations}

A machine restart on the second seed, a twelve-hour gateway outage on the
third whose five poisoned rounds were quarantined and re-flown from the
last verified round, and five meta-chain holes: each is narrated with its
handling in Appendix~\ref{app:ops}, and non-adjacent pairs are left
unpooled. Both arms of both seeds completed sixteen of sixteen
\emph{scored} rounds, and every same-config window used in the flip
statistics is a verified clean pair \F{44}\F{45}.

\subsubsection*{The flip rate replicates}

The same-config flip rate is \textbf{21.0\%} on the first seed
($126/600$ pairs over six full-batch windows, per-window swing
$-4\ldots{+}5$), \textbf{20.1\%} on the second ($161/800$ over eight
windows, swing $-4\ldots{+}2$) --- near-identical to the pair --- and
\textbf{19.2\%} on the third ($115/600$ over six windows, swing
$-8\ldots{+}5$) \F{15}. All three are computed on the same 100-task bed,
so the comparison is between seeds and nothing else. (A seed-level
re-derivation that excludes the restart window gives $20.4\%$ on the
second seed; the third seed's gives $19.5\%$ \F{44}\F{45}.)

Three seeds give $21.0$ / $20.1$ / $19.2$: the figure the readout argument
rests on is stable.

\subsubsection*{The volatile family replicates too}

Flip noise is not uniform across the bed. Tasks that flipped within the
last three rounds re-flip at \textbf{41.6\%} on the first seed,
\textbf{37.7\%} on the second ($49/130$ over five windows), and
\textbf{31.5\%} on the third ($51/162$, six windows) --- $1.6$ to
$2.0\times$ the matching seed's bed-level rate, itself measured on no-graph
windows only \F{41}\F{44}\F{45}.

The flip concentration that makes single-window readings unreadable is
therefore a structural property of the bed, not one campaign's draw.

\subsubsection*{Both arms climb within a campaign}
\label{sec:trend}

Fitting a line to rounds R1--R15 on the common 100-task subset gives a
\textbf{positive slope in all six campaigns} --- no-graph $+0.62$, $+0.44$,
$+0.38$ tasks per round; graph $+0.53$, $+1.39$, $+0.64$. Under a null of no trend, and treating the six campaigns as independent,
six positives out of six is a $1/64$ event, $p \approx 0.016$ \F{47}.
\emph{They are not independent} --- one bed, three seed-pairs, overlapping
calendars \F{59} --- so the level is not claimed and the reading is
descriptive: all three seed-pairs climb, in both arms.

The round-0-to-terminal difference is not that finding. Across the six
arms it correlates with
where round 0 happened to land at $r = -0.87$ \F{46}. That is regression to
the mean on a bed whose same-config swing is $-8\ldots{+}5$: the arms that
``improved most'' are the arms whose first round fell lowest.

One pair was not interleaved. The no-graph seed-1 campaign ran
2026-08-17--18 and its graph counterpart 08-23--27, while the seed-2 and
seed-3 pairs are concurrent with their own counterparts \F{59}. Arm is
therefore confounded with time window on one of the three pairs, against
the first rule of \S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol}; the two concurrent pairs
are what the cross-arm reading rests on.

Between arms, on the common bed: plateau means over R3--R15 are
\textbf{63.4} tasks (no-graph) against \textbf{66.1} (graph), a descriptive
gap of $+2.7$ \F{46}, with its between-seed standard error reported in
Table~\ref{tab:results}, on arms of unequal fresh/carried batch
composition \F{58}; the exploratory within-campaign slope gap is $+0.37$
tasks per round at an exact permutation $p = 0.10$, and the exploratory
last-four-round difference is $+5.25$ at $p = 0.15$ \F{47}. Arm and time
window are also confounded for the non-interleaved seed pair.

\subsection{The gain face: what else clears the band, and what clearing it
             means}
\label{sec:gain-face}

The positive control is one round pair, and alone it would invite a reading
it does not support --- that above-band moves are edits and inside-band
moves are noise. The whole gain face was therefore swept: all $84$
round-to-round pairs that follow the six campaigns' baseline draws, on the
common 100-task bed, against the same-config band $-8\ldots{+}5$ \F{48}.
That band, like Figure~\ref{fig:scores}'s plateau band, is measured on
the no-graph arms' windows only \F{15}\F{9e} and applied here to
graph-arm pairs by the same assumption.
Round 0 is excluded exactly as in \S\ref{sec:trend}: it is a single draw,
it anchors the regression-to-the-mean artifact, and on the first seed it is
the one round scored at $k{=}2$.

Eleven pairs leave the band, all of them upward. \textbf{Six of the eleven
land in rounds where nothing shipped at all.} Five of those six are no-op
audit windows, where only 25 tasks are re-drawn and the delta is a partial
re-draw rather than a round-to-round move; restricting to pairs whose two
rounds both re-ran the whole bed leaves six above-band moves, five
ship-bearing and one not. The five carry candidates from three different
buckets, share no mechanism, and only one of them --- the positive control --- has a
mechanism legible in the exit ledger. The count is caliber-sensitive in
the expected direction: under the $\pm 7$ single-pair ceiling of \F{14}
the whole-bed above-band set is three, all ship-bearing.

\subsection{What the graph did, and what it did not}
\label{sec:what-graph-did}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{Removal is not measurement}

The replacement column saturates by construction under the sliding window:
$549/549$ \F{8}. The false signal's absence is verifiable; the
replacement's presence measures nothing. ``$0/859$'' is therefore not evidence that the graph arm's tool calls were
genuinely used, and is not read that way.

\subsubsection*{Citation integrity improves; the credit goes to the layer}

Digests in the graph arms carry \textbf{3.50} verified citation anchors
each, against \textbf{1.75} in the no-graph arms, while the share of
digests failing the production anchor validator falls from \textbf{11.7\%}
to \textbf{1.5\%} \F{52}. Roughly twice the citing at roughly a seventh of
the failure rate, so this is not the saturation trap of the previous
subsection, where cleanliness is bought by citing less.

Which component earns it is not established. Three GHX changes could each
produce it: the anchor-repair pass, which is an independent flag;
the graph-fed digest itself; and the rebound digest builder. No arm
isolates them.

The defensible claim is that \emph{the GHX layer} improves citation
integrity; that the \emph{graph} does is not shown. The anchor-repair pass
being separately switchable, the gain may be as portable to an unmodified
harness as the cost work of \S\ref{sec:cost}.

\subsubsection*{What the roles read: a vocabulary appears}

The Digester tags each failing dossier with a free-text failure mode. The
no-graph arms' 1{,}585 failing dossiers carry 1{,}431 distinct tags, $96\%$
used once, the three commonest covering $3\%$ --- three spellings of budget
starvation are three tags, so no round can count a family. The graph arms'
1{,}079 dossiers carry 850 tags, still $96\%$ singletons, but the three
commonest cover $17\%$, two of them motif names \F{65}. The graph does not
improve the diagnosis; it gives two rounds a few words in common, which is
the precondition for reading ``this family was treated before''
(\S\ref{sec:archaeology}).


% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Downstream: outcomes do not separate; fewer candidates ship, and
they are cleaner}
\label{sec:localization}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{Localization, and why only one number from it survives}

The loop's own predictions carry real information. On the no-graph
baseline, the tasks a shipped candidate named flipped at \textbf{50.0\%}
against a base rate of \textbf{25.2\%} --- a lift of \textbf{1.99}, with a
95\% confidence interval of $[1.47, 2.54]$ by bootstrap over candidates (13
candidates, 50 eligible predictions), an interval that excludes $1.0$
\F{9a}\F{9d}.

That is the load-bearing localization number in this thesis, and it points
the same way as the archaeology of \S\ref{sec:archaeology}: the base loop
can already aim.

\subsubsection*{The between-arm comparison is withdrawn, not lost}

The graph arm's lift cannot be set beside it. The two shipped sets are
different candidate populations, admitted under different gate regimes ---
five gates against six, killing 18 of 59 candidates ($30.5\%$) against 26
of 55 ($47.3\%$) pooled over three seeds \F{53} --- and drawn
against base pools nine percentage points apart. The graph arm's own
interval includes $1.0$ in any case, so even taken alone the value is
indistinguishable from chance.

Four stacked defects, and the superseded figures they retire, are recorded
at \F{9b}\F{9c}\F{9d}; the body takes no direction from them. A fifth surfaced on the proposal face: the stored core/halo labels use a
broader prediction population than the declared unlock lists and omit
registry-tool touches (\S\ref{sec:proposal-face}, \F{64}).

The design's reach closes the section: a real \textbf{40\%} improvement in
localization would not have been visible at this sample \F{9d}. This is an
\emph{absent measurement} rather than an underpowered null, which would
still point somewhere.

The switch
profit-and-loss account covers 21 items, none of which constitutes a measured
behavioural improvement \F{12}.


The share of candidates dying \emph{malformed} --- failing
canonicalization, replay and structure together --- fell from
\textbf{18/59 ($30.5\%$)} to \textbf{8/55 ($14.5\%$)}, the direct
consequence of both artifacts being machine-generated rather than
transcribed \F{53}.

\paragraph{An alternative reading of the eighteen kills.} An equally
consistent reading is that the cone-based representation itself degrades
targeting rather than revealing targeting that was already bad. The
no-graph arm has no equivalent gate, so its own mistargeting rate is
unmeasured, not zero, and no hand audit of no-graph candidates against the
same criterion was run. The eighteen kills show the gate firing; they do
not by themselves distinguish the two readings.

\subsubsection*{The proposal face: what the graph arm writes}
\label{sec:proposal-face}

The cards the Evolver wrote show what the graph changed before any gate
acted.

\emph{Bucket.} The card's primary declared category is tools for $22\%$ of
86 no-graph cards and $44\%$ of 79 graph cards ---
$20.0/25.0/20.0\%$ against $40.0/48.1/45.5\%$ by seed, disjoint, at the
design's floor of $p = 0.05$ --- while prompt is the primary category for
$22\%$ and $14\%$ \F{61}. The ship-ranking membership counts for tool and
prompt cards are alike in both arms; this is not a census of Critic
acceptance. The sixth gate refuses tools and processors at the same rate,
and the landed sets carry 11 and 13 tool ships \F{62}. Part of it is a rule: the release's own structure gate binds
a candidate to the bucket the Planner flagged, both arms flag tools about
equally often, and only the graph arm's Evolver is told so before writing
--- its tools share is $61\%$ against $18\%$ in flagged rounds, and still
$39\%$ against $23\%$ in the rest \F{62}, the residue the evidence regime
owns.

\emph{Citation.} Both arms cite trajectory steps at six anchors per card;
the graph arm adds a second language --- node names on $65\%$ of cards,
cone and motif terms on $89\%$, lift on $37\%$ --- and drops one, prompt
and template mentions falling from $2.8$ per card on $58\%$ of cards to
$1.0$ on $23\%$ \F{63}. No rule forbids a prompt edit; the cone renders
the prompt as one intervention line and copies no text.

\emph{Prediction.} A graph card declares $5.4$
\texttt{tasks\_will\_unlock} entries against $3.0$ in the no-graph arm
\F{64}. Separately, the stored core/halo labels use a broader prediction
population: the raw labelled core count is $0.9$ tasks per card, with
$4/27$ core and $50/145$ halo hits against a passive $172/530$ \F{64}.
Those labels omit registry-tool touches and do not partition the declared
unlock list, so the counts are implementation diagnostics and do not
estimate the efficacy of cone touch. This population mismatch is the fifth
reason \F{9c} gives for not subtracting the arms' lifts.

\subsubsection*{Why there is no component ablation}

No level-by-level ablation was run. An ablation \emph{attributes} an
arm-level effect to a part, and on the score axis there is no
\emph{separable} arm-level effect to attribute. The reason there are no
per-level arms is simply that no campaign was flown at an intermediate
level (\S\ref{sec:beds}); what such arms would have returned is not
predicted here. What the
thesis substitutes is bookkeeping, labelled \cls{C} observational rather
than offered as a causal decomposition \F{9c}\F{39}\F{40}.

\subsection{Archaeology, the treadmill, and four layers of mortality}
\label{sec:archaeology}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{The loop had already prescribed correctly, four campaigns
earlier}

The finding that reorients the thesis came from re-reading the study's own
archives, and it falsified an earlier claim made here.

Three ancestors, with their decision quotes \F{38}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item In the \emph{zero-graph} baseline arm, round 3, a prompt repair
        carrying a step-cited fabrication diagnosis --- the loop had
        identified a specific fabricated identifier absent from every tool
        output --- accepted ``first in ranking''. This is four campaigns
        before the dossier mechanism that was supposed to make such
        diagnosis possible.
  \item In the graph campaign, round 1, a dormant-rule activation shipping
        with replay counts of 13 fires and 5 false positives on one side,
        9 and 4 on the other; accepted, ship-alone.
  \item An archive-fetch tool, invented by the loop itself.
\end{itemize}

Each family re-surfaces in later campaigns and probes \emph{without
inheriting} from the earlier instance. This is the \textbf{re-prescription
treadmill}: the same drug, re-derived from scratch, repeatedly. Within a campaign the treadmill is visible too: the three graph
seeds' 35 tool cards name 22 distinct tools, three of them re-authored
three times each on one seed, and the no-graph seeds' 19 name 11, one
of them four times \F{67}.

\subsubsection*{Card anatomy: what the graph actually changed}

Diagnosis is native to the base loop: the no-graph cards already reason
from symptom to mechanism to drug on step-pinned quotes --- a fabricated
identifier absent from every tool output, a zero-length return, the prompt
line that sanctioned the behaviour. What the graph changes is the evidence
\emph{regime}: the graph-arm card replays its proposed rule over all 100
recorded execution graphs and ships with fire \emph{and false-positive}
counts \F{38}. Argument quality climbs to readout rung R2, which is still
not R3 --- measured firing sites are death signatures, not cure sites ---
and the localization axis that would have settled the difference is not
measurable here (\S\ref{sec:localization}).

\subsubsection*{The one drug that worked, and where it went}

The budget-starvation repair of \S\ref{sec:one-repair} was retained from
round 3 to round 16 of its own campaign --- a recorded retention example
--- and then reached no later campaign configuration examined \F{39}.

The mechanism is decisive independently of how many campaigns are grepped:
the drug's target is a \texttt{file://} absolute path into its own campaign
directory, and every later seed is generated from the clean template. No cross-seed inheritance channel exists: later campaigns start from the
clean template, so this reset does not demonstrate within-lineage
forgetting.

\subsubsection*{Four layers of mortality} \cls{C}

Why a loop that can prescribe correctly still does not improve, in four
layers \F{40}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Placebo at the capability wall} (primary). The drug is
        correct and the lesion is out of the actuator's reach.
  \item \textbf{Lifetime of at most one campaign.} Seed restarts, the
        three-tier veto, prompt singleton overwrites.
  \item \textbf{Shipped is not firing.} Recorder-gated dormancy, merge
        drops, landing bugs.
  \item \textbf{No readout, therefore no ratchet.}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Supplementary verification}

\subsection{Non-interference, by construction and by demonstration}
\label{sec:non-interference}

The recorder must not change the thing it records.
\emph{By demonstration}: a same-window paired trial on the stable cohort
--- eight always-pass canaries and two never-solved tasks, three
repetitions $\times$ two arms, recording the only difference --- returns 9
of 10 per-task outcome vectors identical, the single divergence being the
documented flaky canary \F{33}, which failed twice in the
recording-\emph{off} arm. Arm totals are $22/30$ against $24/30$, inside noise \F{43}. On a bed that
flips one outcome in five, score identity is not an achievable standard;
vector-level parity on stable tasks is, and is the standard used. That
cohort is chosen for having zero variance (\S\ref{sec:power}), so the
demonstration bounds interference \emph{on stable tasks only}; the 79
volatile tasks, where interference would act, are not covered by it.

\subsection{The efficacy-trial protocol}
\label{sec:efficacy-protocol}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Level 3 of the readout ladder --- did firing change outcomes --- was not
available from the loop, so it was built by hand, following the
\emph{add-on} design of clinical-trial methodology~\cite{ich-e10}: the
graph mechanism is added on top of the base loop and read against the
base loop alone, rather than either arm forgoing a standard-of-care
control. The protocol below is what a manual R3 readout costs, and serves
as a specification for the automated version \S\ref{sec:future} calls
for.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Arms are interleaved within the same window.} Running arms
        serially confounds arm with time window; its absence destroyed one
        result here \F{36}.
  \item \textbf{True completions are counted before anything is scored.}
        Launch-spike deaths and phantom runs are excluded by inspection.
        This is registry error 14.
  \item \textbf{Campaign task parameters are replicated exactly.} The
        campaigns run \texttt{--max-steps 20}; the loader's default of 40
        doubles the step budget and revives tasks that had died of burnout,
        which silently changes what is being measured. Registry error 15.
  \item \textbf{Adjudication files are quarantined outside the
        subject-reachable filesystem, and seeded with canary words.} If a
        planted marker word ever surfaces in a subject trajectory, the
        subject read what it should not have. Registry error 13 is the
        specimen: an adjudication file left readable in a run root, a role
        that read the answer sheet, and a round of rulings voided.
  \item \textbf{Gold labels are health-checked before trials}, following
        the archaeology protocol --- a wrong gold label was found this way
        \F{29}.
  \item \textbf{A canary set of eight always-pass tasks detects harm}
        \F{33}, stopping arithmetic decides continuation, and
        \textbf{mechanism endpoints are reported beside scores}: firing
        forensics, and the confident-wrong share --- the fraction of
        failures in which the subject closed confidently on a wrong
        answer.
\end{itemize}

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Multiplicity, declared}

There is \textbf{exactly one confirmatory test} in this thesis: the
\emph{prospective} efficacy batch of $n{=}70$ pairs --- 7 clean tasks
$\times$ 10 repetitions $\times$ 2 arms --- which is the test the stopping
rule was fixed against \F{34}. The pooled 174-pair reading is a
\emph{supporting} estimate over all clean same-window pairs, reported
because both point estimates are negative and neither separates --- they
differ in magnitude, $-8$ of 70 against $-3$ of 174 --- and not because it
was pre-specified.

No external registry was used, so what \emph{pre-specified} rests on is
stated here. The cluster-transfer and canary designs, with their
$\pm 3$ floor and their sample sizes, were written into the working notes
and committed to the author's working repository before their data
existed (25 August 2026, 01:30; the trial files are stamped 02:04--02:14)
\F{32}\F{33}. The rules of the prospective batch --- seven fixed clean
tasks, one of them a known negative case that could not be dropped, a
sign test, at most two batches --- were written in the working notes
before the batch was launched, but their committed record was made
together with the verdict, after the batch had run. That batch's
pre-specification therefore rests on the notes, not on a timestamp
\F{34}. The log excerpts and the plan text are reproduced in the released
repository under \texttt{docs/provenance/}.

Every other $p$-value in this document is exploratory. That includes the
McNemar window of Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} \F{21}, the same-window controls
\F{36}, the notes channel \F{35}, the cluster transfer \F{32}, and the
within-campaign slope permutation of \S\ref{sec:trend} \F{47}. Each is
labelled exploratory at its point of use as well as here, and no
family-wise correction is claimed.

Four successive apparent wins were executed one at a time and killed by
three different instruments; the two whose magnitudes have ledger rows are
$+7$ and $+5$ \F{36}. They are serial re-tests of
a single hypothesis, which is why the confirmatory endpoint is the
pre-specified prospective batch and none of them.

\subsection{Evaluator-bias controls}
\label{sec:evaluator-bias}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{pass@1 discipline.} Mixed-$k$ passes do not count. Applying this
rule mechanically to the baseline campaign's seed round moved its recorded
score from 67 to \textbf{57}: $k$-mixing had gifted the seed ten free
passes (registry error 11, Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). That round is
excluded from every flip and gain-face statistic for this reason
\F{15}\F{48}; Table~\ref{tab:arms} prints its uncorrected R0 because the
R0 column reports what each campaign recorded, and no reading rests on
it.

The direction of the correction matters: lowering the seed \emph{enlarges}
the campaign's apparent gain, so the rule, applied mechanically in both
directions, happens here to flatter this thesis's own baseline campaign.

\subsection{The clinic: capability against efficacy}
\label{sec:clinic}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The campaigns answer whether the graph changes outcomes. They do not answer
whether the loop is \emph{able} to diagnose, because a campaign confounds
ability with everything else in the loop. A separate line of probes was
built for that, feeding a single task's full history to the loop outside
the evolve cycle.

\subsubsection*{The capability chain is intact end to end}

$k{=}1$ observations suffice for the loop to reach the correct drug family.
More trajectories buy argument quality, claim-narrowing and honest
abstention --- not new families \F{26}--\F{30}. Fed a cluster of related
failures, the loop returns one general prescription, narrows its own claim,
and abstains where it should \F{36}.

Pushed with a coverage directive --- treat every task, never abstain ---
the loop's rational optimum is to engineer the bed's own leak routes into a
benchmark-lookup cheat tool, which passed the Critic and \emph{all} landing
gates \F{37}. It was not shipped for scoring; the text evidence is
retained. \S\ref{sec:governance} reads it as the governance result it is.

Every item above is capability evidence; none of it is efficacy evidence.



Three drugs the loop repeatedly prescribes were assembled into a
configuration bundle and run through the pre-specified trial protocol of
\S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Clean-cluster transfer: $+1$ and $+2$, inside the $\pm3$ floor ---
        engaged but unreadable \F{32}.

  \item \textbf{Verdict on the pre-specified test: no improvement, with a
        negative point estimate} \F{34}. This is the observed
        decision-rule result, not an equivalence claim. The
        two readings are kept apart. The prospective batch --- 7 clean
        tasks $\times$ 10 repetitions $\times$ 2 arms, $n{=}70$ pairs ---
        returns $+1/-4$, sign test $p = 0.97$. The pooled clean
        same-window pairs ($n{=}174$) return 75 for the parent against 72
        for the applied configuration, one-sided Fisher $p = 0.67$. And
        the mechanism endpoint, confident-wrong share, is flat in both.
\end{itemize}

The bundle trial detected no improvement and returned a negative point
estimate, with the named mechanism endpoint flat
(\S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol}). This is an interpretable null for the
specified decision rule, not an equivalence result: it does not
establish a true zero or exclude smaller effects. The post-hoc control
event is an observation of a large event rather than a calibration of
sensitivity to smaller effects.

\subsection{The survivor}
\label{sec:survivor}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

One positive channel survived the whole study. Episodic notes ---
answer-scrubbed per-task history, prepended directly to the task and
bypassing the evolve loop entirely --- returned $22/72$ against a bare
$17/72$: $+5$ passes over 72 evaluations; at task level, 3 of the 6 tasks
rose and 1 fell, not significant \F{35}. The largest gains fell where the
note was most on-target. Deciding its fate needs three or four more window
pairs, so it is reported as unresolved.

Its interest is structural rather than numerical: it is the only channel
that bypasses all four mortality layers of \S\ref{sec:archaeology} --- it
is injected per task rather than occupying the prompt singleton slot, never
enters the evolve loop, cannot go dormant because it is prepended to the
task itself, and needs no efficacy signal because the operator decides its
life or death.

The price of that immunity is that it is no longer self-evolution but
retrieval-based experience reuse, the adjacent literature line
\S\ref{sec:graph-memory} sets aside: the one channel that worked is the one
that gave up the property under study.

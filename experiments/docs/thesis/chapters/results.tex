\section{Scores and measurement design}

\subsection{Beds, arms, and the switch ladder}
\label{sec:beds}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The bed is the \textbf{100-task no-pixel subset} of GAIA's text-only
split, for the four reasons \S\ref{sec:what-was-run} gives. The three seeds
are replications within each treatment arm under the same task and model
settings.


The \emph{no-graph} arm is the official system with every GHX flag off.
The \emph{graph} arm is the same system with the \textbf{whole stack on}
--- recording, graph evidence, graph verification and the typed candidate
surface together. The switches are cumulative by construction, each level
keeping the ones below, but no campaign was flown at an intermediate
level, which is why \S\ref{sec:localization} reports no component
ablation: there are no per-level arms to attribute an effect to.

Earlier graph campaigns
flown before the graph-layer fixes are excluded by a scope ruling
(\S\ref{sec:scope}, Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}). Scoring is performed
by the structurally hardened LLM judge of \S\ref{sec:measurement} \F{69},
and every between-arm comparison here uses audited scores only \F{46}.

All cross-arm comparisons use the same 100-task GAIA evaluation set
\F{46}. Three of those
100 are never solved in 9{,}600 task-round cells and contribute no variance, so
every reported \emph{difference} is identical on 97 or on 100 \F{54}; the
100-task denominator is kept regardless, since dropping tasks on the basis
of their outcomes is selection.

\subsubsection*{Models}

Every campaign runs the same two models through a LiteLLM gateway, and the
runbooks record the pair on every launch line: the \textbf{solver} is
\texttt{deepseek-v4-flash} (284B parameters, 13B active; thinking enabled,
effort high), and the \textbf{four meta-roles and the judge} are
\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro} (1.6T, 49B active). The official paper's one
complete experiment has the same shape --- a stronger meta tier over a
weaker task tier, Claude Opus over Sonnet, GPT and Qwen --- on a different
benchmark, so the comparison across the two is one of form, not of
number.

The split follows the published model cards, fixed before any campaign
flew.\footnote{DeepSeek-V4 model cards, Hugging Face, accessed 4 September
2026 (\url{https://huggingface.co/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4-Flash}), and the
API pricing page (\url{https://deepseek.ai/pricing}): BrowseComp Pass@1
V4-Pro Max $83.4$, V4-Flash High $53.5$; MRCR-1M V4-Flash Non-Think
$37.5$; list price per million tokens \$0.14 / \$0.28 against \$0.435 /
\$0.87.} Pro on the solver would have flattened the window a harness
effect has to show in --- it scores $83.4$ on BrowseComp, the published
benchmark closest to this bed, near the open-model ceiling --- where Flash
sits mid-curve at $53.5$ and costs a third as much, which is what makes
the three-seed replication of \S\ref{sec:replication} affordable at all.
Flash's one documented hard limit, long-context collapse with thinking
off, is avoided: thinking is on in every trajectory here.  Claims are scoped to this pair; no claim in this thesis
concerns model tiers.

\subsection{Power arithmetic}
\label{sec:power}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{The flip rate}

Take two consecutive rounds in which nothing shipped. The configuration is
identical; the only difference is that the bed was run again. Across the
six such windows in the baseline campaign's sixteen-round readout,
\textbf{21.0\%} of task outcomes flip --- $126$ of $600$ task-pairs, 95\%
Wilson interval~\cite{wilson-1927} $[17.9\%, 24.4\%]$ \F{15}. The other two seeds give
$20.1\%$ and $19.2\%$, and pooled over all twenty windows the figure is
\textbf{20.1\%} on $402/2{,}000$ pairs, $[18.4\%, 21.9\%]$ \F{9e}. A
task-clustered bootstrap~\cite{efron-1979} --- resampling the 100 tasks rather than the pairs,
with each task carrying all of its windows --- widens this to $[16.5\%,
23.9\%]$ \F{57}. It preserves within-task windows but does not adjust for
shocks shared across tasks in a window. Similar point estimates recur
across runs of this measured bed--solver--harness--judge--environment setup;
they are not attributed to the benchmark alone. This is also the scope of the ledger's ``bed'' shorthand.

Over those windows, $52.3\%$ of task-campaign pairs flip at least once \F{15}.
The resulting total-score swing for a single window runs from $-8$ to
$+5$ tasks \F{15}\F{9e}.

\subsubsection*{The arithmetic}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item One fifth of task outcomes flip on re-measurement with no treatment
        applied.
  \item The bed therefore swings by up to eight tasks between two
        identical rounds.
\end{enumerate}



\subsubsection*{One envelope, one caliber}

The swing is quoted throughout as an \emph{observed range} over ship-free
windows, and the range depends on how many windows were drawn:
$-4\ldots{+}5$ on seed 1 (6 windows), $-4\ldots{+}2$ on seed 2 (8
windows), and $-8\ldots{+}5$ on seed 3 (6 windows), all on the
config-identity window rule this thesis quotes throughout
\F{15}\F{9e}\F{44}\F{45}.
The descriptive reference range used throughout this thesis is the union,
$\mathbf{-8\ldots{+}5}$ tasks, quoted that way everywhere; per-seed
ranges appear only where a seed is being described. All twenty windows
are no-graph windows: the graph arm's no-op rounds were audit batches
(Appendix~\ref{app:cost}), so it contributes no whole-bed same-config window, and a
graph-arm pair read against the band is read on the assumption that its
noise is the same (\S\ref{sec:threats}).

A range over a handful of windows is not a standard deviation. Across the
twenty adjacent same-configuration windows, the sample SD of paired score
changes is $\mathbf{3.70}$ tasks; three times that descriptive scale is
$11.1$ \F{15}. Neither quantity is a formal detection threshold or a
per-round score SD. The one number on
which the two calibers disagree is the $+9$ of \S\ref{sec:one-repair},
discussed there and in \S\ref{sec:readout}.

\subsubsection*{Historical stability does not supply a natural-noise estimate}

The natural response to a noisy bed is to find its reliable core and
measure there. The census forecloses it. Across the six whitelist campaigns on the 100-task subset ---
9{,}600 task-round cells, of which 8{,}276 are fresh draws and the
remaining 1{,}324 carried \F{51}\F{58} --- \textbf{3} tasks are never solved,
\textbf{18} always pass, and \textbf{79 are volatile} \F{54}.

The 21 historically stable tasks contribute no observed variance under
these campaign configurations, so they do not estimate re-draw variability;
that history does not exclude harm or benefit under a new intervention. The
79 volatile tasks supply the observed natural variation.

Bed reliability here cannot be bought by item selection. It can be bought
in repetitions --- a Spearman--Brown calculation~\cite{spearman-1910,brown-1910} prices that against the
measured per-task flip rate --- or by changing the readout, which is this
thesis's own route (\S\ref{sec:future}). Under F60's independent-Bernoulli, fixed-propensity model, a single draw's
reliability is $0.57$, and \textbf{four} averaged repeats are the first
integer count reaching a conventional $0.80$ reliability bar \F{60}. This model-based
price does not guarantee whole-bed reliability \F{60}.

\subsection{Model-conditional resolution sensitivity}
\label{sec:resolution}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\emph{Resolution} here names the model-conditional detection-sensitivity
calculations below, not a threshold supplied by the observed band and not
the metrology sense of an instrument's smallest distinguishable increment
(VIM). For this measured setup, \F{34} motivates the sensitivity analysis:
one task's observed base rate spans $\pm 50$ points on a half-hour scale,
while a single-window gain of up to five tasks is insufficient, by itself,
to attribute an improvement.

\subsubsection*{The swing is already there at a single instant}

Every reading above measures the bed twice with a scheduling window in
between. One simultaneous twin window establishes draw-level variation, but
resolves neither a between-window drift component nor dependence among task
outcomes or repeated draws \F{70}. Its two draws score \textbf{61} and
\textbf{57}, and 16 of 100 tasks disagree, 95\% Wilson $[10.1,24.4]$;
the adjacent-window rates of $21.0\%$ \F{15} and $20.1\%$ \F{9e} lie inside
that interval.

For sensitivity analysis only, suppose task-level evaluation errors are
conditionally independent and repeated draws are independent Bernoulli trials
at fixed task propensities, transferring the no-graph window's variance model
to both arms. No graph whole-bed same-configuration window tests that equality.
Under this untested model the 16\% disagreement gives a per-window score SD of
$2.83$ tasks and a two-window paired-change SD of $4.00$; the directly
observed $3.70$ over twenty adjacent windows is descriptively close
\F{70}\F{15}. The two predicted SDs depend on this model; their proximity
to the directly observed scale does not validate its independence assumptions.

\subsubsection*{Conditional repetition sensitivity}

Under the same independence model, averaging $k$ repeats per task gives
an MDE of $11.2/\sqrt{k}$ tasks at $80\%$ power \F{70}:

Under this model the recorded $+2.7$-task arm-mean difference corresponds
to $k\approx17$ \F{70}. The no-graph arm logged 39 whole-bed plateau rounds;
the graph arm logged 24, plus 15 audit batches that re-drew 25 tasks and
carried the rest \F{58}. If 39 rounds had instead been independent repeats
of one fixed configuration under the model, the MDE would be $1.8$ tasks;
the between-seed calculation is $10.4$ \F{70}\F{46}.

The campaigns did not supply those fixed-configuration repeats: configurations
evolved across rounds, and graph audit rounds carried outcomes. The effective
replication for the reported arm summary is three seeds. Evolution and precise
contemporaneous measurement therefore competed for the available budget, but
the data identify neither a factor-six loss nor the resolution repetition
would recover under the flown dependence structure. Campaign-dashboard nulls
must be read with that model dependence in view.

Every same-config window used in the flip statistics is a verified clean
pair: a round pair in which both rounds re-ran the whole bed --- a
\emph{full batch}, as against the partial audit batch of
Appendix~\ref{app:cost} --- with no ship between its rounds and

\subsubsection*{The flip rate replicates}

The same-config flip rate is \textbf{21.0\%} on the first seed
($126/600$ pairs over six full-batch windows, per-window swing
$-4\ldots{+}5$), \textbf{20.1\%} on the second ($161/800$ over eight
windows, swing $-4\ldots{+}2$) --- near-identical to the pair --- and
\textbf{19.2\%} on the third ($115/600$ over six windows, swing
$-8\ldots{+}5$) \F{15}. All three are computed on the same 100-task bed,
so the comparison is between seeds and nothing else.

Across these seeds, the recorded flip pattern replicates within the measured
bed--solver--harness--judge--environment setup.

\subsubsection*{The volatile family replicates too}

Flip noise is not uniform across the bed. Tasks that flipped within the
last three rounds re-flip at \textbf{41.6\%} on the first seed,
\textbf{37.7\%} on the second ($49/130$ over five windows), and
\textbf{31.5\%} on the third ($51/162$, six windows) --- $1.6$ to
$2.0\times$ the matching seed's bed-level rate \F{41}. The ratio is cross-arm:
its numerator is measured in the graph campaigns' audit windows, its
denominator on no-graph whole-bed windows \F{41}\F{44}\F{45}.

The flip concentration therefore recurs across campaigns of this measured
bed--solver--harness--judge--environment setup.

\subsubsection*{Recorded campaign dashboards rise}
\label{sec:trend}

Fitting a line to rounds R1--R15 on the common 100-task subset gives a
\textbf{positive slope in all six campaigns} --- no-graph $+0.62$, $+0.44$,
$+0.38$ tasks per round; graph $+0.53$, $+1.39$, $+0.64$ \F{47}. Six of six
positive is not a test result: the campaigns share one bed and overlapping
calendars \F{59}, and the fits start at a trough, which makes a positive
slope the expectation without any drift; the reading is descriptive: all
three seed-pairs climb, in both arms.

The fits start at a trough. R1 is the lowest round of the fifteen in five
of the six campaigns (Table~\ref{tab:rounds}), on two graph seeds it is
itself a 25-task audit batch carrying the single R0 draw forward on 75
tasks, and the one early move with a legible mechanism, the guard of
\S\ref{sec:one-repair}, is a single step that a straight line reads as
slope. Refit from R3 the slopes are no-graph $+0.08/+0.18/+0.49$ and graph
$+0.39/+1.41/+0.59$, still six of six positive; on the graph arm's
full-batch rounds only they are $+0.20/+1.41/-0.04$ from R1 and
$-0.12/+1.29/+0.50$ from R3, so the arm's higher mean slope rests on one
seed, which climbs from 53 to 72 across nine ships that no per-edit
readout can attribute \F{47}. The residual climb, under a task per round in five campaigns and about $1.4$ on the sixth, is
consistent with a cumulative real gain of that size and with its absence
(\S\ref{sec:proposition}).

The round-0-to-terminal difference is not that finding. Across the six arms, the pass@1 round-0 score correlates with
terminal-minus-round-0 gain at $r=-0.85$ \F{46}. Because round 0 is also subtracted inside the gain, that
correlation is mathematically coupled and does not establish a trend.

One pair was not interleaved. The no-graph seed-1 campaign ran
2026-08-17--18 and its graph counterpart 08-23--27, while the seed-2 and
seed-3 pairs are concurrent with their own counterparts \F{59}. Both
seed-1 campaigns ran the same model names through the same gateway under
byte-identical harness configuration apart from the GHX flags, and no
backend change is on record for the interval; the calendar is reported
as a fact of the record, and all three pairs enter the cross-arm means.

As operational dashboard summaries, the plateau means over R3--R15 are
\textbf{63.4} tasks (no-graph) and \textbf{66.1} (graph), a recorded gap of
$+2.7$ whose standard error, intervals and minimum detectable difference
are in Table~\ref{tab:results} \F{46}; by seed the gap is $-2.4/+0.9/+9.5$,
and without seed 3, the pair whose gradings the outage of
\S\ref{sec:threats} degraded, it is $-0.75$ \F{46}\F{69}. The arms use
unequal fresh/carried measurement policies \F{58}. The arm-mean slope difference of
Table~\ref{tab:results} is read against an exact three-versus-three
permutation over seeds; that test and the archived last-four-round
diagnostic are exploratory \F{47}. These quantities are not common contemporaneous score
contrasts, and no arm-separation inference is drawn.

\subsection{The gain face: what else clears the band, and what clearing it
             means}
\label{sec:gain-face}

The mechanism-consistent guard event is one round pair, and alone it would invite a reading
it does not support --- that above-band moves are edits and inside-band
moves are noise. The whole gain face was therefore swept: all $84$
round-to-round pairs that follow the six campaigns' baseline draws, on the
common 100-task bed, against the same-config band $-8\ldots{+}5$ \F{48}.
That band, like Figure~\ref{fig:scores}'s plateau band, is measured on
the no-graph arms' windows only \F{15}\F{9e} and applied here to
graph-arm pairs by the same assumption.
Round 0 is excluded exactly as in \S\ref{sec:trend}: it is a single draw,
it anchors the regression-to-the-mean artifact, and on the first seed it is
the one round scored at $k{=}2$ --- two attempts per task, where the
thesis's convention is pass@1 (\S\ref{sec:evaluator-bias},
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).

Eleven pairs leave the band, all of them upward --- under a symmetric $\pm 5$
caliber it would be eleven up against four down, so the one-sidedness rests
on the band's lower edge \F{48}. \textbf{Two of the eleven
land in rounds where nothing shipped.} Five of the eleven are no-op audit
windows, where only 25 tasks are re-drawn and the delta is a partial
re-draw rather than a round-to-round move; restricting to pairs whose two
rounds both re-ran the whole bed leaves six above-band moves, five
ship-bearing and one not. The five carry candidates from three different
buckets, share no mechanism, and only one of them --- the
mechanism-consistent guard event --- has a mechanism legible in the exit
ledger; none isolates one candidate's efficacy. As an illustrative independent-pair reference calculation, a zero-mean

\subsection{What the graph did, and what it did not}
\label{sec:what-graph-did}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{Removal is not measurement}

The column the graph arm puts in place of the construction-mismatched ``output used''
verdict --- results entering later context, counted against results read
by an answering call --- saturates by construction under the sliding
window: $549/549$ \F{8}. The construction-mismatched proxy's absence is verifiable; the
replacement's presence measures nothing. ``$0/859$'' is therefore not
evidence that the graph arm's tool calls were genuinely used, and is not read that way \F{7}.

\subsubsection*{Citation integrity differs across arms; components are not isolated}

Digests in the graph arms carry \textbf{3.50} verified citation anchors
each, against \textbf{1.76} in the no-graph arms, while the share of
digests failing the production anchor validator falls from \textbf{11.6\%}
to \textbf{1.5\%} \F{52}. Roughly twice the citing at roughly a seventh of
the failure rate, so this is not the saturation trap of the previous
subsection, where cleanliness is bought by citing less.

Three GHX changes accompany this whole-stack association: the
anchor-repair pass, which is an independent flag; the graph-fed digest
itself; and the rebound digest builder. No arm isolates their effects.

Over the graph campaign's early failed cohort ($n{=}56$, rounds R0--R1),
the median unfolded graph $U$ is a 1{,}186-node, 606{,}000-character record;
its full cone is \textbf{48 nodes} and its data cone \textbf{42}, about
$4\%$ of the invocation's nodes \F{55}. In the rendered-context measurement, both reported
sizes are medians: the rendered cone is \textbf{15.5\,kB} and the trajectory
with which it shares a context budget is \textbf{87.4\,kB} ($n{=}44$, R1)
\F{55}. The rendered-byte ratio recorded in the ledger is not interpreted
here because its aggregation is undocumented.

The defensible claim is a whole-stack association: graph-arm digests have
more verified anchors and fewer validator failures across all three observed
seed pairs. The design does not identify a GHX-layer, graph-evidence, or
component effect; the arm/time and downstream digest-population limitations
still apply. The separately switchable anchor-repair
pass may be as portable to an unmodified harness as the cost work of
\S\ref{sec:cost}.

\subsubsection*{What the roles read: a vocabulary appears}

The Digester tags each failing dossier with a free-text failure mode. The
no-graph arms' 1{,}585 failing dossiers carry 1{,}431 distinct tags, $96\%$
used once, the three commonest covering $3\%$ --- three spellings of budget
starvation are three tags, so no round can count a family \F{65}. The graph arms'
1{,}079 dossiers carry 850 tags, still $96\%$ singletons, but the three
commonest cover $17\%$, two of them motif names \F{65}. These tag counts do not establish improved diagnosis. They show shared
terminology across rounds, which could support recognition of recurring
failure families (\S\ref{sec:archaeology}).

\subsection{Candidate behavior and localization under the two-arm design}
\label{sec:localization}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{Localization, and why only one number from it survives}

Among the baseline campaign's 14 landed candidates, 13 are eligible for
the defined unlock estimand and supply 50 predictions: C-R13-01 declares no
unlocks and only stabilization/pass predictions. These eligible predictions
form a descriptive association: named tasks flipped
at \textbf{50.0\%} against a base rate of \textbf{25.2\%}, a lift of
\textbf{1.99} \F{9a}. Separately, the graph arm's within-arm figure is
$0.78$ on nine shipped candidates, and $1.23$ on the two that survive the
strictest window \F{9b}. Each figure describes its own arm's selected
population, and no arm contrast is formed from them \F{9c}. The candidate-bootstrap interval in \F{9d} is an
archived calculation, not calibrated inferential support: candidate rows
share task and round outcomes and base pools, while its numerator and
denominator use different resampling units.

\subsubsection*{Interpretation of localization evidence}

The arm-specific localization diagnostics describe associations between
candidate predictions and task outcomes within each arm's selected candidate
population. Graph evidence was introduced together with verification, typed
candidate operations and an additional gate, so this design does not isolate
the graph-evidence component specified by H1. The candidate populations also
differ: five gates against six, rejecting 18 of 59 candidates ($30.5\%$) against 26
of 55 ($47.3\%$) pooled over three seeds \F{53}, and base pools
nine percentage points apart. The graph-arm diagnostic has limited resolution
and is inconclusive; the no-graph lift remains a descriptive selected-candidate
association.

The calculation and measurement limitations are documented with the
superseded figures in \F{9b}\F{9c}\F{9d}. A further limitation concerns
candidate definitions: the stored core/halo labels use a broader prediction
population than the declared unlock lists and omit registry-tool touches
(\S\ref{sec:proposal-face}, \F{64}).

The switch-level
cost and capability account covers 21 items, none of which identifies an isolated behavioural effect \F{12}.


The share rejected as \emph{malformed at the recorded gates} ---
failing canonicalization, replay and structure together --- fell from
\textbf{18/59 ($30.5\%$)} to \textbf{8/55 ($14.5\%$)} \F{53}. This
recorded-gate observation excludes the four later baseline-compose
refusals. It compares different candidate populations;
it does not isolate artifact generation as the cause of the change.

\paragraph{An alternative interpretation of the graph arm's eighteen
sixth-gate rejections, pooled over three seeds.} An equally
consistent reading is that the cone-based representation itself degrades
targeting rather than revealing targeting that was already bad. The
no-graph arm has no equivalent gate; the same criterion applied to its
landed candidates' own landing rounds finds 3 of 14 processor candidates
that never intervened and 0 of 10 tools that never ran \F{71}. The eighteen
rejections show the gate firing; they do not by themselves distinguish the
two readings.

As a retrospective stress test, the gate's criterion flags 6 of 22 selected
shipped tool candidates with a drawable task: 4 of 9 in the no-graph arm and
2 of 13 in the graph arm, whose 13 are post-gate survivors \F{71}. These
selected populations do not estimate the false-refusal probability among the
18 candidates actually refused. Within this retrospective sample, requiring
absence on two draws instead of one flags 2 of 22. One of the 18 refusals
names a tool the drawn task called five times once a re-proposed card landed.

\subsubsection*{Proposal behaviour across the two arms}
\label{sec:proposal-face}

The candidate cards show a change in proposal allocation before any gate
acts. The no-graph mix is led by processor proposals, whereas tools are the
largest category in the graph arm. The primary declared category is tools
for $22\%$ of 86 no-graph cards and $44\%$ of 79 graph cards ---
$20.0/25.0/20.0\%$ against $40.0/48.1/45.5\%$ by seed, disjoint, so an
exact one-sided permutation over the three seeds per arm returns
$p = 0.05$ (exploratory), the smallest value that design can return --- while prompt is
the primary category for $22\%$ and $14\%$ \F{61}. These categories mark different intervention
sites. Processor proposals change lifecycle components around model and tool
execution; tool proposals change a registered tool implementation. Both are
available in the released harness, so the shift describes where the Evolver
places proposed changes rather than a new action surface.

The difference is already present at authorship. Ship-ranking membership for
tool and prompt cards is similar in the two arms, but ranking membership is
not Critic acceptance. The landed sets contain 11 and 13 tool ships \F{62};
those counts describe later filtering and cannot be equated with proposal
counts or effectiveness. The additional gate refuses candidates in both the
tool and processor categories, so its decisions do not by themselves explain
the authorship mix.

Planner-to-Evolver bucket feedback is one plausible contributor. The
released structure rule requires a candidate to agree with the bucket that
the Planner flagged in both arms, while only the graph arm's Evolver receives
that bucket explicitly before writing. In rounds where tools are flagged, its tools share is
$61\%$ against $18\%$, and in the remaining rounds it is $39\%$ against
$23\%$ \F{62}. The larger contrast where the signal is supplied is
compatible with an explicit relay from planning to proposal authorship. It
remains a whole-stack association: the design does not isolate that relay
from typed operations, graph evidence, verification, or the other arm
changes.

The evidence language changes alongside allocation. Both arms cite about six
trajectory steps per card. Graph cards additionally use node names on
$65\%$ of cards, cone and motif terms on $89\%$, and lift on $37\%$;
prompt and template mentions fall from $2.8$ per card on $58\%$ of cards to
$1.0$ on $23\%$ \F{63}. Addressable nodes and cones expose processors and
tools as operational targets, while the prompt appears as one intervention
line without its text. This representation can plausibly redirect attention
toward named runtime components, but the vocabulary counts do not establish
more accurate diagnosis. Consistent with that local emphasis, insertion and
parameter-mutation operations dominate the typed proposals, while the
available restructuring operations are unused. The linked graphs expose processors and recorded execution, yet the
observed proposals remain predominantly local edits.

Graph cards also declare broader intended effects: a card lists $5.4$
\texttt{tasks\_will\_unlock} entries against $3.0$ in the no-graph arm
\F{64}. The separate stored core/halo diagnostic uses a broader prediction
population: the raw labelled core count is $0.9$ tasks per card, with
$4/27$ core and $50/145$ halo hits against a passive $172/530$ \F{64}.
It omits registry-tool touches, a material limitation when the proposal mix
is tool-oriented, and it does not partition the declared unlock list. These
figures therefore show broader declared ambition and a different bookkeeping
surface; they do not estimate a reach gain, cone-touch efficacy, or a graph
component effect. This population mismatch is the fifth reason \F{9c} gives
for not subtracting the arms' lifts.

Both configurations nevertheless author concrete interventions. The graph
arm changes their allocation and representation, but the evidence does not
show a graph-exclusive repair class or superior effectiveness. The following
archival analysis therefore treats autonomous proposal generation as a
capability present across the study lineage and asks what happens after a
proposal is authored.

\subsubsection*{Why there is no component ablation}

No level-by-level ablation was run. An ablation \emph{attributes} an
arm-level effect to a part; no campaign was flown at an intermediate
level (\S\ref{sec:beds}), so the whitelist holds no per-level arm to
attribute anything to, and what such arms would have returned is not
predicted here. What the
thesis substitutes is bookkeeping, labelled \cls{C} observational rather
than offered as a causal decomposition \F{9c}\F{39}\F{40}.

\subsection{Repeated candidates and stage-specific limitations}
\label{sec:archaeology}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection*{Autonomous proposal capability in baseline and graph-enabled runs}

Archived candidate and decision records show that autonomous proposal
generation was present in the released baseline and remained present when
graph evidence was added. Here \emph{autonomous} means that the loop authored
and reviewed a concrete candidate; it does not establish task repair or an
outcome benefit. Three specimens locate that capability across the study
lineage \F{38}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item In the no-graph baseline campaign, round 3, the loop authored a
        step-cited prompt repair for a fabricated identifier absent from the
        tool record, and the Critic accepted it ``first in ranking''. This is
        four campaigns before the later dossier mechanism.
  \item In the earlier partial graph campaign, round 1, the loop authored a
        dormant-rule activation and replayed it offline over the recorded
        round-0 graphs, reporting where the rule fired and where it produced
        false positives; the Critic accepted it to ship alone. This is an
        archival capability specimen, not an arm result from the later
        whitelist comparison; its counts lie outside the evidence base and
        are not reported.
  \item In the whitelist graph campaign, the loop authored an archive-fetch
        tool.
\end{itemize}

Together the three specimens show autonomous proposal capability in both
configurations.

Each family re-surfaces in later campaigns and probes \emph{without
inheriting} from the earlier instance. This is a pattern of \textbf{repeated independent proposals}: the same
intervention family is derived from scratch more than once. Repeated proposals also occur within campaigns: the three graph
seeds' 35 tool cards name 10, 6 and 6 distinct tools per seed, three of
them re-authored three times each on one seed, and the no-graph seeds' 19
name 4, 5 and 2, one of them four times \F{67}.

\subsubsection*{Card anatomy: evidence supporting proposals}

Both configurations can move from a recorded symptom to a proposed mechanism
and intervention. The baseline card uses step-pinned trajectory evidence for
its diagnosis. The earlier graph-enabled card adds offline replay over all
100 recorded execution graphs, recording where its proposed rule fired and
where it produced false positives; those counts lie outside the evidence
base and are not reported here \F{38}. This replay provides
readout rung R2, which is still not R3: it identifies where a rule applies,
not where it changes outcomes. The archived localization diagnostics do not
identify the graph-evidence component effect
(\S\ref{sec:localization}). A loop-authored specimen rule fired 19 times
across 10 tasks, with zero observed collateral damage and zero conversions
in this recorded sample \F{11}\F{20}; this records an end-to-end live channel
without estimating an outcome effect.

\subsubsection*{The guard event and what became of it}

The step-countdown processor of \S\ref{sec:one-repair} stayed in its
campaign's configuration for R3--R6 only and left at R7, after which exits
rose again; the same processor family reappears in three graph campaigns
by independent re-proposal, not inheritance \F{39}\F{38}.

The mechanism is decisive independently of how many campaigns are grepped:
the intervention's target is a \texttt{file://} absolute path into its own
campaign directory, and every later seed is generated from the clean
template. No cross-seed inheritance channel therefore exists, and the
reset this produces does not demonstrate within-lineage forgetting.

\subsubsection*{Four latent failure explanations} \cls{C}

Four latent explanations for why repeated proposals may not produce improvement, in four
layers \F{40}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Capability limit.} A candidate may be ineffective because
        its target lies outside the actuator's reach.
  \item \textbf{Retention scope.} Clean-template seed restarts are a
        design property; within campaigns, vetoes and singleton overwrites
        may limit residence.
  \item \textbf{Shipped is not firing.} Recorder-gated dormancy, merge
        drops and landing bugs may break delivery.
  \item \textbf{Readout limit.} Treatment-specific efficacy is not wired
        into retention. These are coexisting Class-C explanations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Supplementary verification}

\subsection{Non-interference, by construction and by demonstration}
\label{sec:non-interference}

The recorder must not change the thing it records.
\emph{By demonstration}: a same-window paired trial on the stable cohort
--- eight always-pass canaries and two never-solved tasks, three
repetitions $\times$ two arms, recording the only difference --- returns 9
of 10 per-task outcome vectors identical, the single divergence being the
documented flaky canary \F{33}, which failed twice in the
recording-\emph{off} arm. Arm totals are $22/30$ against $24/30$, a
two-pass difference confined to that canary \F{43}. Aggregate score equality
alone would not establish non-interference; this check instead reports
per-task outcome vectors. That
cohort is chosen for having zero variance (\S\ref{sec:power}), so the
demonstration bounds interference \emph{on stable tasks only}; the 79
volatile tasks, where interference would act, are not covered by it.

\subsection{The efficacy-trial protocol}
\label{sec:efficacy-protocol}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Rung R3 of the readout ladder --- did firing change outcomes --- was not
available from the loop, so it was built by hand, by analogy with the \emph{add-on} structure in
clinical-trial methodology~\cite{ich-e10}. The
treatment is a fixed bundle of three loop-generated, gated candidates
composed into the external HarnessX archive's applied preset
\texttt{PROBE\_DOSSIER6/config\_verify\_applied.yaml}, read against its
inherited parent \texttt{config\_verify\_parent.yaml}. It replaces the
parent prompt with \texttt{gaia\_verify\_first.md}, which instructs
verification of unsupported answers from task start. The
\texttt{SearchLoopSteeringProcessor} acts only when repeated
\texttt{WebSearch} crosses its preset threshold; the \texttt{ArchiveFetch}
replacement acts when called and retries transient retrieval failures. The
preset and referenced code fix their parameters and code paths. The parent
retains its prior prompt and tool and lacks the steering processor. This
estimates the fixed bundle as a bundle; it does not attribute the result to a
component, graph evidence, GHX as a whole, or any exact archaeology ancestor.
The archive supports loop generation, gating and composition, but does not
establish generation through a separate typed graph-edit API. The proof
summaries are linked to the presets by arm and session names and tracer
destinations, but do not embed a configuration hash. The protocol below is
what a manual R3 readout costs,
and serves as a specification for the automated version
\S\ref{sec:future} calls for.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Arms are interleaved within the same window.} Running arms
        serially confounds arm with time window; its absence destroyed one
        result here \F{36}.
  \item \textbf{True completions are counted before anything is scored.}
        Launch-spike deaths and phantom runs are excluded by inspection.
  \item \textbf{Campaign task parameters are replicated exactly.} The
        campaigns run \texttt{--max-steps 20}; the loader's default of 40
        doubles the step budget and revives tasks that had exited after budget exhaustion,
        which silently changes what is being measured.
  \item \textbf{Adjudication files are quarantined outside the
        subject-reachable filesystem, and seeded with canary words.} If a
        planted marker word ever surfaces in a subject trajectory, the
        subject read what it should not have. The specimen behind this
        rule is an adjudication file left readable in a run root, a role
        that read the answer sheet, and a round of rulings re-run in
        isolation.
  \item \textbf{Gold labels are health-checked before trials}, following
        the archaeology protocol --- a wrong gold label was found this way
        \F{29}.
  \item \textbf{A canary set of eight always-pass tasks detects harm}
        \F{33}, stopping arithmetic decides continuation, and
        \textbf{mechanism endpoints are reported beside scores}: firing
        forensics, and the confident-wrong share --- the fraction of
        failures in which the subject closed confidently on a wrong
        answer.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Multiplicity, declared}

There is \textbf{exactly one confirmatory test} in this thesis: the
\emph{prospective} efficacy batch of $n{=}70$ execution pairs --- 7 clean
tasks $\times$ 10 repetitions $\times$ 2 arms \F{34}. Its inferential unit is the
task-level aggregate: seven task differences, of which five non-tied signs
enter the sign test fixed in the stopping rule \F{34}. The pooled 174-pair
reading remains descriptive: the negative totals, $-8$ of 70 against $-3$
of 174, are archived, while its Fisher calculation ignores pairing and task
clustering and is not a supporting confirmatory test \F{34}.

No external registry was used, so what \emph{pre-specified} rests on is
stated here. The cluster-transfer and canary designs, with their
$\pm 3$ floor and their sample sizes, were written into the working notes
and committed to the author's working repository before their data
existed, the record being reproduced in \texttt{docs/provenance/}
\F{32}\F{33}. The rules of the prospective batch --- seven fixed clean
tasks, one of them a known negative case that could not be dropped, a
sign test, at most two batches --- were written in the working notes
before the batch was launched, but their committed record was made
together with the verdict, after the batch had run. That batch's
pre-specification therefore rests on the notes, not on a timestamp
\F{34}. The log excerpts and the plan text are reproduced in the released
repository under \texttt{docs/provenance/}.

Every other $p$-value in this document is exploratory. That includes the
McNemar~\cite{mcnemar-1947} window of Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} \F{21}, the same-window controls
\F{36}, the notes channel \F{35}, the cluster transfer \F{32}, and the
within-campaign slope permutation of \S\ref{sec:trend} \F{47}. Each is
labelled exploratory at its point of use as well as here, and no
family-wise correction is claimed.

Four successive apparent positive results were executed one at a time,
each rejected by a different check \F{34}; the two whose magnitudes have ledger rows are
$+7$ and $+5$ \F{36}. They are serial re-tests of
a single hypothesis, which is why the confirmatory endpoint is the
pre-specified prospective batch and none of them.

\subsection{Evaluator-bias controls}
\label{sec:evaluator-bias}

\paragraph{Single-draw discipline.} Every score in this thesis is pass@1,

\subsection{Capability and efficacy probes}
\label{sec:clinic}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The campaigns do not answer whether the loop is \emph{able} to diagnose,
because a campaign confounds ability with everything else in the loop. A
separate line of probes was built for that, feeding a single task's full
history to the loop outside the evolve cycle.

\subsubsection*{Demonstrated diagnosis and proposal capability}

$k{=}1$ observations suffice for the loop to identify the relevant intervention family \F{38}.
More trajectories are associated with more detailed arguments, narrower claims,
and appropriate abstention --- not new families \F{26}--\F{30}. Fed a cluster of related
failures, the loop returns one general intervention proposal, narrows its own claim,
and abstains where it should \F{36}.

Pushed with a coverage directive --- treat every task, never abstain ---
the loop engineered the bed's own leak routes into a
benchmark-lookup cheat tool, which passed the Critic and \emph{all} landing
gates \F{37}. It was not shipped for scoring; the text evidence is
retained. \S\ref{sec:governance} reads it as the governance result it is.

Every item above is capability evidence; none of it is efficacy evidence.



The fixed bundle of \S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol} supplies the treatment
evaluated below.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Clean-cluster transfer: $+1$ and $+2$, inside the $\pm3$ floor ---
        engaged but unreadable \F{32}.

  \item \textbf{Verdict on the pre-specified test: no improvement, with a
        negative point estimate} \F{34}. This is the observed
        decision-rule result, not an equivalence claim. The
        two readings are kept apart. The prospective batch --- 7 clean
        tasks $\times$ 10 repetitions $\times$ 2 arms, $n{=}70$ execution
        pairs --- returns $+1/-4$, sign test $p = 0.97$ \F{34}. Repetitions are
        aggregated into seven task-level differences, with five non-tied
        signs entering that test. The pooled clean archive reading
        ($n{=}174$) returns 75 for the parent against 72 for the applied
        configuration, one-sided Fisher $p = 0.67$; that calculation
        ignores pairing and task clustering \F{34}. And
        the mechanism endpoint, confident-wrong share, is flat in both.
\end{itemize}

The bundle trial detected no improvement and returned a negative point
estimate, with the named mechanism endpoint flat
(\S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol}). This is an interpretable null for the
specified decision rule, not an equivalence result: it does not
establish a true zero or exclude smaller effects. The post-hoc guard
event is mechanism-consistent but lacks a same-window counterfactual
and concurrent-ship isolation.



% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The episodic-note result}
\label{sec:survivor}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

One channel produced a positive but unresolved result. Episodic notes ---
answer-scrubbed per-task history, prepended directly to the task and
bypassing the evolve loop entirely --- returned $22/72$ against a bare
$17/72$: $+5$ passes over 72 evaluations; at task level, 3 of the 6 tasks
rose and 1 fell, not significant \F{35}. The largest gains fell where the
note was most on-target. Resolving this result requires three or four additional window
pairs, so it is reported as unresolved.

Its interest is structural rather than numerical: it bypasses the
harness-evolution mechanisms discussed in \S\ref{sec:archaeology} --- it
is injected per task rather than occupying the prompt singleton slot, never
enters the evolve loop, cannot go dormant because it is prepended to the
task itself, and the operator makes the retention decision.

This channel is retrieval-based experience reuse rather than
self-evolution, the adjacent literature line
\S\ref{sec:graph-memory} sets aside: the positive result therefore concerns a different
mechanism class from the property under study.

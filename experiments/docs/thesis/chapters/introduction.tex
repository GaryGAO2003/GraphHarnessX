\chapter{Introduction}
\label{ch:intro}
% =========================================================================

\claim{On this bed, a released single-lineage self-evolving harness exposes
a construction mismatch in its evidence column. GHX replaces that evidence
with a graph-native record and typed candidate operations; the whole-stack
two-arm outcomes are descriptive, H1's localization endpoint is not identified
by this design, and a separately protocolled bundle trial reports no detected
improvement with a negative point estimate. These observations motivate a
conditional readout hypothesis without excluding a capability limit.}

In this chapter and throughout the thesis, score summaries support no
common-endpoint arm contrast or separation inference in either direction;
the measurement-policy caveat behind that reading is stated once, in
Table~\ref{tab:results}.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The system under study}
\label{sec:motivation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

An agent is a model plus a harness, and the harness is frequently the
dominant performance lever. That makes the harness a natural target for
automatic optimization, and a literature of self-evolving agents has grown
around the idea~\cite{survey-selfevolve}. Most of it evolves prompts,
workflows or agent code, and by 2026 the harness itself has a line of its
own --- attribution-guided harness repair, gated promotion, harnesses as
editable documents (\S\ref{sec:self-evolving}). GraphHarnessX, the system
built for this thesis and introduced in \S\ref{sec:ghx-preview}, implements
the integration studied here: a native execution graph, cross-task
aggregation, persisted writeback and residence across rounds, with typed
candidate operations making changes inspectable (\S\ref{sec:positioning}).

HarnessX/AEGIS~\cite{aegis} is one of that line, and is the system this
thesis takes as its object. Its four named meta-agent stages are bracketed
by task execution and deterministic gating, run here on
DeepSeek models served through a LiteLLM gateway --- the weaker tier on
the solver, the stronger on the four meta-roles and the judge
(\S\ref{sec:beds}). Each round the
solver attempts the bed under a fixed configuration. A \textbf{Digester}
reads every trajectory --- failures and successes alike; the success
template instructs it to identify the reusable strategy and any latent
fragility --- and writes a per-task dossier. A
\textbf{Planner} synthesizes the dossiers into one landscape. An
\textbf{Evolver} authors zero or more candidate edits to the harness's
configuration, tools, prompt or processors, each as a manifest plus an
applied configuration. A \textbf{Critic} judges each candidate, may
interrogate the Evolver, and audits the candidates as a set against the
scoreboard. Candidates retained by the Critic face five deterministic gates in their execution order ---
structure, novelty, canonicalization, a counterfactual replay over the
previous round's passing tasks, and a synthetic-task smoke
(\S\ref{sec:official-strengths}).
Figure~\ref{fig:official} is the system's own picture of itself;
Figure~\ref{fig:loop} opens its \emph{adapt} vertex into the six operational phases
just described and marks the seams at which Chapter~\ref{ch:ghx}
attaches.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{figures/harnessx_architecture.jpg}

The implemented gates make the system suitable for studying which
properties its validation process can audit.

\subsection*{The release against the paper}

The paper's GAIA headline is a $+13.6$-point gain that appears only once
variants are isolated inside an ensemble~\cite{aegis}, and it is the
difference between two peaks rather than a gain over a starting point: the
paper's own single-lineage arm peaks at $73.8\%$ and then \emph{ends at}
$49.5\%$ --- a $24.3$-point collapse its own figure attributes to rollback
failing --- while the ensemble ends at $87.4\%$. No round-zero figure is
published for either \F{68}. The released code is that single lineage: the
variant pool and ensemble routing the headline rests on are absent, leaving
a \texttt{ship\,|\,no\_op} decision on one configuration, the arm whose
published trajectory is the collapsing one.

The headline is therefore not recomputable from the release. What was run
is the released system, not the system the paper describes; the
distinction is maintained throughout, and every divergence between the two
is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}.

\section{What was run, and why this bed}
\label{sec:what-was-run}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The released system was vendored byte-for-byte under a sha256 manifest
enforced by tests and executed in the no-graph campaigns. The complete
comparative study also includes GHX whole-stack campaigns: six claim-bearing
campaigns on the \textbf{100-task no-pixel bed} drawn from the text-only
split of GAIA~\cite{gaia} --- one bed for every number in this thesis, and
two \emph{arms}, the no-graph arm and the graph arm (\S\ref{sec:beds}).
Each campaign is read over its formal sixteen-round window, so
\textbf{96 rounds and 8{,}276 fresh task evaluations} enter the complete
study evidence base \F{51}. Independent execution of the released system is
scoped to the no-graph campaigns.

\subsection*{Why GAIA, and why these hundred tasks}

Four reasons.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It is the authors' bed.} The paper's headline is a GAIA
        number and its own narrative describes a GAIA pilot, so a GAIA bed
        is the one on which the released system can be held to its
        paper.
  \item \textbf{The text-only split keeps the solver inside what the
        recorder can attribute.} GAIA's text-only tasks are answered
        through tools the harness owns --- shell, file reads, web search
        --- so every step of a trajectory is an event the execution graph
        of Chapter~\ref{ch:ghx} can record. Three of the split's 103 tasks
        carry pixel content, and the DeepSeek models serving the solver
        accept no multimodal input, so those three cannot be attempted;
        they are excluded, which leaves 100. Every number in this thesis
        is on those 100 (\S\ref{sec:beds}).
  \item \textbf{There is room.} Across the six campaigns the union of
        tasks ever solved is 97 of the 100, against a best single round of
        76: twenty-one tasks of headroom that the loop's own history shows
        to be reachable, and 47 tasks whose pass rate stays below $80\%$
        \F{16}. The bed is neither saturated nor floor-bound.
  \item \textbf{It is small enough to support repeated whole-bed re-runs.} A
        hundred tasks can be attempted in full sixteen times per campaign,
        which is what makes same-configuration re-measurement
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:design}) affordable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What running the baseline revealed}
\label{sec:baseline-revealed}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The loop's central evidence signal --- whether a tool call's output was
\emph{used} --- has a construction mismatch, which follows from its
own construction.

The column grades a call by whether the immediately next assistant message
shares a verbatim substring of at least twenty characters with the tool
output. The rule cannot credit verbatim reuse confined to spans shorter than
twenty characters. Among the 419 overlap-rule-negative answer-carrying calls,
the reference-answer median is nine characters \F{3}; this selected diagnostic
subset is not a bed-level prevalence or a measured false-negative rate.

The remaining baseline observations do not establish an edit effect: a
plateau whose climb is under a task per round \F{47}, an edit ledger that
fixes one thing and breaks another, and shipped-edit margins that mostly lie
within the observed same-configuration swing (score movement seen when an
unchanged configuration is run again; Chapter~\ref{ch:design}). The
above-range moves are swept in \S\ref{sec:gain-face}; only the guard event of
\S\ref{sec:one-repair} has a legible mechanism \F{48}. This produced the
hypothesis.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{H1.} The loop's failure is an \emph{evidence} problem: faithful
execution-level causal evidence will improve edit localization and
outcomes.
\end{quote}

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The hypothesis and its literature}
\label{sec:hypothesis}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------


H1 names two endpoints, edit localization and outcomes; the study addressed
localization first, and no pre-registration record fixes either as primary.
The whole-stack design did not identify the graph-evidence component
estimand --- the quantity a design is built to estimate --- behind
localization, so that endpoint remains untested; the reason is developed in
\S\ref{sec:readout}.

\subsection*{Research questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

Three questions organize the study; the design answers them with different limits.

\paragraph{Q1, diagnosis.}\label{rq:1} How is the released loop's evidence
column constructed, and how far does its verdict reach into candidate
decisions? The evidence column has a construction mismatch. In the
baseline campaign, eligible predictions from selected shipped candidates
show a descriptive within-arm association: lift $1.99$ from the observed
rates \F{9a}. The archived candidate-bootstrap interval is not used as
calibrated inferential support, and the between-arm aiming comparison is not
adjudicable (Q3, \F{9c}); retention must be read against observed same-configuration variation. Q1 was posed from
what running the baseline campaign revealed
(\S\ref{sec:baseline-revealed}), before H1 existed to test.

\paragraph{Q2, non-interference.}\label{rq:2} Does enabling the recorder
alter observed task outcomes in the tested cohort? The recording comparison is
limited to a selected stable cohort; the volatile remainder is not covered
(\S\ref{sec:non-interference}). It therefore supplies a bounded
non-interference observation rather than a whole-bed guarantee.

\paragraph{Q3, the effect of graph evidence.}\label{rq:3} What is the
effect of supplying graph evidence on edit localization while holding the
other stack elements and admission rule fixed? The estimand is that
graph-evidence component effect. The whole-stack design
does not identify that component effect, and its arm-specific
post-selection localization diagnostics are inconclusive for it. A second
quantity, the whole-stack contrast between the two packages on one bed, is
what the two-arm design was built around; the recorded rows do not deliver
it as a common-endpoint contrast (Table~\ref{tab:results}), and the
full-batch row that equalizes the measurement policy is read at a precision
of about ten tasks; it compares unequal selected round sets under changing
configurations and partially non-contemporaneous calendars \F{58}\F{59}, so no
separation inference is drawn in either direction.

\section{GraphHarnessX, in one paragraph}
\label{sec:ghx-preview}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

GraphHarnessX --- GHX for the rest of this document --- attaches to the
byte-pinned official substrate from outside and links a typed exported-
processor composition graph $G$ to an observed execution DAG $U$. The
composition graph materializes exported hooks, processors and slots and
orders represented processors at byte-level parity; ungraphed processors
retain their explicit legacy-order path. Typed candidate operations make
processor changes inspectable, while tool-registry changes remain
inspectable in generated configuration and the edit ledger. The unfolded
execution DAG records runtime tool calls and nested-agent events; its data
edges are cross-checked against runtime slot provenance and never invented,
carrying
causal ancestor cones (over-approximations of what could have shaped a
failure, never counterfactuals). The pair is what makes rungs R1 and R2 of
\S\ref{sec:readout} mechanical: the composition graph locates
processor-graph targets, while the execution DAG says whether they ran and
what they fired on. Four switches are stacked cumulatively --- recording,
then graph evidence, then graph verification, then typed candidate
operations --- each keeping the ones below; the campaigns reported
here fly the whole stack against the stack switched off, not one level at
a time (\S\ref{sec:beds}).

Chapter~\ref{ch:ghx} is the system; Chapter~\ref{ch:design} is how it was
tested.

\section{Summary of findings}
\label{sec:verdict}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The principal findings are summarized here and reported in full in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Observed execution and checks.} The construction-mismatched proxy is gone from every
        dossier of the whitelist graph campaign \F{7}; the graph gates have live firing records
        \F{11}; and the loop itself began using the graph \F{38}\F{20}.
  \item \textbf{The score dashboards are not a common-endpoint arm
        contrast.} On the common task set, the recorded arm-mean plateaus differ by $+2.7$ tasks against a between-seed
        standard error of $2.8$ \F{46}. The no-graph plateau is entirely
        fresh while the graph plateau includes selectively carried outcomes,
        so these summaries support no separation inference in either
        direction. The design
        supplies no identified measurement of H1's graph-evidence component
        effect; the arm-specific post-selection localization diagnostics are
        inconclusive for that estimand \F{9c}\F{9d}.
  \item \textbf{Landing and rejection counts.}
        Landed candidates per round are $0.60$, $0.73$ and $0.60$ on the
        three graph-arm seeds against $0.93$, $0.73$ and $0.80$ on their
        no-graph counterparts \F{10}: no graph seed ships more. The graph arm's
        sixth gate rejected 18 candidates, pooled over the three graph seeds,
        that had cleared all five official gates but named a target absent or,
        for a processor, non-intervening in the one selected failure replayed;
        four distinct no-graph refusals occurred later at baseline compose
        \F{53}. A retrospective
        application flags 6 of 22 selected shipped
        tools, a selected-sample outcome rather than an estimate of actual
        false-refusal probability \F{71}. Separately, the
        recorded-gate observation shows that the share rejected as malformed at
        those gates fell by half; it does not isolate the contribution of
        artifact generation \F{53}.
  \item \textbf{The candidate surface changed shape}, not reach. Proposals
        became typed candidate operations committed atomically with machine-generated
        artifacts, and the loop authored a runtime rule through the
        graph-fed channel that cleared the offline replay gate \F{20}\F{38}.
        The official free-text path could already install a processor, and
        did \F{39}.
  \item \textbf{A pre-specified paired efficacy trial finds no improvement, with a
        negative point estimate}; this is the observed decision-rule result,
        with the mechanism endpoint flat while the treatment engages \F{34}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What the design says about H1.} Both H1 endpoints concern
graph-evidence component effects, which the whole-stack design does not
identify. The score summaries \F{14}\F{46}, the archaeology of repeated
proposals \F{38} and the fixed-bundle trial \F{34} each answer a different
question, and none identifies H1's component effects. The trial rules were
written before launch and committed with the verdict \F{34}, its
cluster-transfer and canary designs before their data \F{32}\F{33}; the
archaeology is post-hoc \F{38}.

\section{The deeper finding: readout and actuator jointly unresolved}
\label{sec:readout}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The intervention families later proposed by the evidence-fed loop had already been
proposed --- by the official loop, in the no-graph arm, on
a construction-mismatched evidence column, from single observations, four
campaigns earlier \F{38}. Each family re-surfaces later without
inheriting from the earlier instance, forming a pattern of \textbf{repeated
independent proposals}.

One mechanism-consistent event pairs a fall in budget-exhaustion exits from
$35$ to $4$ with a $+9$ score movement; two other candidates shipped into the
same round, no same-window counterfactual isolates the step-countdown
processor, and it left the configuration four rounds later \F{39}.
The gain-face sweep shows that band crossing cannot isolate edit efficacy
\F{48}. Separately, across four selected whole-bed pairs, movement in the
observed starvation-count proxy did not consistently track score movement
\F{49}. This bounded
observation does not estimate the ordering of latent intervention effects;
a score null remains ambiguous. \S\ref{sec:gain-face} carries the sweeps and
\S\ref{sec:proposition} the conditional argument.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Self-evolution $=$ variation $+$ selection $+$ readout.}
\end{quote}

In the released loop, aggregate score is a retention signal. On this bed,
same-configuration re-measurement flips $21.0$, $20.1$ and $19.2\%$ of task
outcomes across three seeds \F{15}\F{9e}. If selected edit effects are small
relative to that readout variation, score-based retention may mis-rank them. The
proposition is about effect detectability under noise, not about
observability of execution; it sits beside, not above, the rival account
that the targeted failures may be beyond what the available actions can repair \F{40}, which
\S\ref{sec:proposition} weighs rather than dismisses. Its constructive
counterpart is the four-rung readout ladder of \S\ref{sec:ladder} ---
\textbf{R0} deployed, \textbf{R1} would have executed on the failures it
cites, \textbf{R2} fired, \textbf{R3} changed outcomes --- which grades
evidence about an edit, not the GHX switches L0--L5 of
\S\ref{sec:ghx-preview}. R0 to R2 live in GHX \F{11}\F{38}; R3 was built
and exercised by hand here \F{34}\F{36}. No verified treatment-specific
efficacy-to-retention link was identified among the implementations
reviewed here.

\section{Scope and claim discipline}
\label{sec:scope}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Comparisons are arm-level; no single-factor attribution is made \F{9c}.
The localization lifts are arm-specific, selected-population diagnostics.
They are reported separately and do not identify the graph-evidence
component effect \F{9a}\F{9b}\F{9c}.

One bed, one solver tier, three seeds per arm \F{44}\F{45}; audited scores
only.

\paragraph{Data scope.} Claim-bearing campaign evidence excludes runs
predating the baseline campaign (early commissioning, before the system was
properly built) and the two GHX-shakedown campaigns. It rests on the baseline
campaign, the post-fix graph campaign, the two replication pairs
\F{44}\F{45}, and pre-specified probes. The sole exception is F38's labelled
archival candidate-and-decision specimen, which supports retrospective
proposal capability only; its firing, outcome and localization measurements
remain excluded from campaign results and comparisons.

\paragraph{Timing of the evidence-scope ruling.} The evidence whitelist was
fixed after the excluded campaigns had run and been read. It invalidated the
project's most graph-favourable arm-level localization reading;
Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} records the ruling and its direction.

\paragraph{Judgment.} Judgment-typed claims are labelled \cls{C}; withdrawn
readings are not re-cited.

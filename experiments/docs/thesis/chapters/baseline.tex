\section{What the official loop does well, and where the release diverges}
\label{sec:official-strengths}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four of the loop's design choices were kept or built on here, and the
diagnosis that follows should be read against them.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It digests successes.} The all-pass Digester template
        instructs the role ``not to celebrate'' but to name the reusable
        strategy and any latent fragility. Most systems read only failures.
  \item \textbf{It invokes itself selectively}, on an actionability score
        computed mechanically from the dossier mix, and \textbf{every role
        runs behind scope gates} that forbid it the harness source, other
        runs' histories, and absolute paths inside shell commands --- a
        defence this thesis's own registry error 13
        (\S\ref{sec:governance}) shows to be necessary.
  \item \textbf{Five deterministic gates, cheapest first}
        (\S\ref{sec:motivation}), of which the structure gate's twelve
        evidence invariants and the counterfactual replay are the ancestors
        of this thesis's sixth gate.
  \item \textbf{Shipped candidates are held to their own predictions.}
        Each names the tasks it expects to flip; the next round scores that
        prediction, and a hit rate below one half triggers rollback. This
        is the ancestor of the localization metric of
        \S\ref{sec:localization}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Where the release diverges from the paper}

Five specifications in the paper are absent from the code: the variant
pool and ensemble routing behind the GAIA headline (the release is one
lineage with a \texttt{ship\,|\,no\_op} decision); the global ratchet
forbidding a solved task to be given back; the $\pm 5\%$ noise threshold on
retention; three seeds per cell, the code defaulting to $k{=}1$; and the
Planner's per-candidate briefs. Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} records each
with its code pin.

The measures taken are the same in every case. The tree is vendored
byte-for-byte under a hash manifest, so what was run is the release and not
a repair of it; the authors' own upstream fix is adopted where they
published one; the missing specifications are supplied by the
\emph{experiment} rather than by patching the system --- three seeds per
arm, a pre-specified noise band, offline regression accounts --- and every
statistic in this thesis is recomputed from raw trajectories rather than
read from the loop's own output. Where a finding depends on one of these
divergences, the dependence is stated.

\section{The system as fielded}
\label{sec:fielded}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each round is bounded by a token and cost budget and by a cap on the
number of tasks attempted. Two properties of the architecture
(\S\ref{sec:motivation}) matter for everything that follows.

The first is that \textbf{the loop edits itself through free text}. The
Evolver does not emit a typed patch; it composes a manifest and a
\texttt{config.yaml} as prose, and whatever survives that transcription is
the edit that actually lands. The limitation is not the edit \emph{surface} --- it is wide, and
\S\ref{sec:one-repair} shows the loop installing a new processor class
through it --- but the edit's \emph{identity}: nothing downstream can
confirm that the candidate document and the applied change describe the
same intervention.

The second is that \textbf{the loop is a single lineage}. There is one
configuration, and it evolves round by round. There is no population, no
archive of alternatives, and --- despite what the paper specifies --- no
global ratchet guaranteeing that a task solved in round $k$ is still solved
in round $k+1$. A candidate that lands and is wrong has no floor beneath it;
\S\ref{sec:open-loop} gives what guards the position instead.

\section{Open-loop hazards: feedback written but not delivered}
\label{sec:open-loop}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

A self-evolving loop is only closed if the consequences of an edit reach
the author of the next edit. Four channels break that delivery, and all
four are properties of the released tree rather than of the campaign flown
or the model tier configured.

\paragraph{The regression report is left empty by a round-indexing slip.}
Each round the loop computes which previously-passing tasks have started
failing --- the signal a lineage without a ratchet most needs. An
off-by-one in the round index draws the comparison against an empty set,
so the report is structurally always empty. The authors' own upstream fix
is adopted here and every regression account is re-derived offline.

\paragraph{The verdict contract is stated twice, and the two statements
have drifted apart.} The schema a role is instructed to emit and the schema
the consumer validates against no longer agree, so validation fails
softly: the outcome is dropped rather than raised, and the next author is
never told what happened to the last edit.

\paragraph{The safety clamp cannot engage on a small bed.} Rollback
fires only when the pass \emph{rate} drops by at least $0.05$ \emph{and}
the pass \emph{count} drops by at least $3$ against the last validated
round (\texttt{run\_meta\_aegis.py:1425}). The conjunction is a property
of the code: wherever fewer than three tasks were passing, the count
condition cannot be met at all, and where exactly three were, only a
total loss meets it. The clamp is arithmetically unreachable in exactly
the situation --- new wiring, first exercised on a handful of tasks ---
for which a clamp exists.

\paragraph{A single lineage with no ratchet, guarded by a sentence.} The
paper specifies a global monotone ratchet: no previously solved task may be
given back. The code implements no such thing. What it has instead is an
advisory watchlist over adjacent rounds, which the agent may overrule by
declaring a regression transient; a counterfactual replay gate; and a
post-hoc rollback triggered when a shipped candidate's prediction hit rate
falls below $0.5$. None of these is a floor. In flight the watchlist fires at the noise rate --- hard regressions
before 81 of 86 evolve rounds across the six whitelist campaigns --- and
the rollback rule never fired (\S\ref{sec:archaeology}, \F{66}).

The tree guards its own worst known regression --- an Evolver edit that
replaced the system-prompt builder and thereby deleted a 23K-character
injected domain policy, recorded in the authors' own words as a
``catastrophic regression\ldots dropped 55\%'' --- with an instruction
inside a prompt template rather than a code path, and the only committed
performance number in the repository is a commit message recording that
two lineages \emph{never recovered} from one such crash \F{19}. A loop
that cannot un-poison its own lineage is the case for attaching a readout
to the retention decision (\S\ref{sec:future}).

\section{The measurement layer: three contamination sources}
\label{sec:measurement}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------


\paragraph{Judge false positives can flip the sign of a round.} Scoring is
performed by an LLM judge, and an unhardened judge's false positives are
large enough to reverse the sign of a round-over-round delta. The judge
used here is structurally hardened, and \emph{every} between-arm comparison
in this thesis uses audited scores only.

\paragraph{The bed's ground truth is partly on the public web.} Of a
49-task cohort assembled from the tasks that swing, thirteen are
\emph{leak-route}: more than half of their passes touch benchmark
artifacts, and reference answers appear verbatim inside solver
trajectories \F{31}.

\paragraph{Re-measuring the same configuration moves 20\% of the bed.}
This finding governs the design of every experiment in this thesis; the
arithmetic is worked in \S\ref{sec:power}. Across the six ship-free windows of this
campaign's sixteen-round readout, $21.0\%$ of task outcomes flip between
two consecutive rounds running an identical configuration --- $126$ of
$600$ task-pairs --- and the resulting total-score swing runs from $-4$ to
$+5$ with no treatment applied at all \F{15}.

The third source is not a defect hardening removes: it is a property of
the instrument and replicates across three seeds
(\S\ref{sec:replication}).

\section{The evidence-pipeline audit}
\label{sec:evidence-audit}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every dossier the Digester produces carries, for each tool call, a verdict
on whether the call's output was \emph{used}. That verdict is the loop's
only signal about whether an action mattered. It is consumed by the
Planner when ranking directions, quoted by the Evolver when justifying a
candidate, and reviewed by the Critic when deciding whether to veto. Across
the baseline campaign's sixteen scored rounds, the column judges only
$8.1\%$ of payload calls to have been used \F{1}. The defect is in the
column, and is established in three tiers of decreasing strength; the
argument needs only the first two, the third resting on a premise the data
cannot supply.

\subsection*{(i) Construction}

The column tests whether the \emph{immediately next} assistant message
shares a verbatim substring of at least twenty characters with the tool
output (\texttt{harnessx/\allowbreak aegis/\allowbreak stages/\allowbreak trace\_facts.py}). Three kinds of
genuine use are therefore graded ``not referenced'' by construction, with
no data required to see it: use that is paraphrased rather than quoted; use
that occurs later than the next step; and use of any fact shorter than
twenty characters.

\subsection*{(ii) Blindness on this bed}

The third of those is not a corner case here. GAIA final answers have a
median length of \textbf{9} characters and a 90th percentile of
\textbf{14} \F{3} --- both below the column's own floor.



\subsection*{(iii) Rate, and what it does not license}

The rate measures the reach of the defect. Of the $559$ calls whose output literally
contains the final answer of a rollout that passed, \textbf{419
($75.0\%$)} receive the overlap rule's ``not referenced'' label, and
$96.7\%$ of those carry an answer below the column's own minimum length
\F{2}. The figure is robust
across variants of the answer-length window at $73.8$--$75.1\%$ \F{2};
the column's own twenty-character threshold was not swept, and by tier (i)
it could not have been swept without changing the instrument.

The limit: a call whose output contains the answer, on a path that passed,
is a call sitting on the successful path holding the answer, which is not
the same as having caused the pass. The finding
therefore rests on (i) and (ii), which need no causal premise, and the
$75.0\%$ is reported as a property of the column's output --- the rate at
which it applies its overlap verdict to answer-carrying calls --- not as a
count of verified causal errors.

\subsection*{The disclaimer, and why it makes the case worse}

The Digester's own text does carry a caveat. It reads:

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Heuristic:} assistant message after the tool call does not contain
any $\ge$20-char substring from the tool output. This is the single most
common way a tool call ``fires but fails to affect the model'' --- e.g.\
when protocol flattening drops multimodal content.
\end{quote}

The disclaimer names one failure mode --- multimodal flattening --- and
never mentions that \emph{any answer under
twenty characters is invisible to it}, on a bed whose median answer is
nine. It describes the column as detecting calls that fire without
affecting the model, which is precisely the interpretation tier (ii)
forbids. And it does not travel with the verdict: the per-call rows in the
dossier's main table carry a bare \textbf{NO}.

\subsection*{Reach into decisions}

The verdict propagates. It reaches \textbf{86.0\%} of dossiers \F{4}, it
is quoted verbatim inside three candidate documents \F{5}, and one
candidate was shipped on it \F{6}.

That shipped candidate named three tasks it expected to flip and hit none
of them. \emph{This is reported as illustration, not as inference.} Against
the round's base rate of $7/32$, a candidate with no effect whatever
returns zero of three with probability $0.48$. The specimen shows how a
false column propagated into a shipped decision; it says nothing about
whether that decision was good. The same evidentiary standard that
withdraws an $n{=}9$ comparison in \S\ref{sec:localization} applies with
more force at $n{=}1$, and is applied here.

\section{Decision-plane pathologies}
\label{sec:decision}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two further properties of the decision plane were recorded; both are
code-pinned.

\paragraph{The cause-of-death ledger is written and never read.} The loop
records why each candidate died; nothing consumes it. Write sites and
absent read sites are both given in Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}. This is the
fourth broken delivery channel of \S\ref{sec:open-loop}.

\paragraph{The aimer is never shown where its aims landed.} The guidance
rebind list omits the Planner (\texttt{planner.py:30--46}), in every arm,
so the role that decides what to aim at never sees where previous aims
landed: whatever the loop learns about which targets convert does not reach
the stage that chooses targets.

\section{The one real repair}
\label{sec:one-repair}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

One intervention in the baseline campaign demonstrably worked. It matters
not for its size but because it is the only event that calibrates the
instrument.

A shell-quoting bug in the harness's Bash tool
caused commands to consume the round's token budget without producing
useful output, so tasks were dying of budget starvation rather than of
anything to do with their difficulty. The loop diagnosed this, authored a
guard processor, and shipped it. In the round that followed,
\texttt{budget\_exceeded} exits collapsed from $35$ to $4$; twelve of the
sixteen tasks that newly passed had died of starvation in the round before;
the GAIA difficulty tier 2 subset gained $16.3$ percentage points; and the total score
rose by $9$ tasks, clearing the same-config band \F{39}. It is the largest
single-round move that campaign makes after its baseline draw, and --- of
the six above-band whole-bed moves in all six campaigns --- the only one
whose mechanism is legible in the exit ledger \F{48}.

The two calibers disagree, and the disagreement is not resolved in this
thesis's favour: a range drawn from six windows is not a standard
deviation, and the paired-change sample SD of $3.70$ in \S\ref{sec:power}
does not define a score-level sigma. The repair stands on the mechanism
counts under either caliber; what remains unresolved is how small an effect
the score axis can register.

\paragraph{How to read it.} This is an engineering-defect repair, not a
capability lift. The loop did not become better at solving GAIA tasks; it
stopped wasting the budget it needed to solve them. It is also a recorded example of a large, mechanism-confirmed event
being retained: the intervention was kept for the rest of the campaign. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} returns to it twice: once to
show that it never reached a later campaign, the inheritance channel not
existing \F{39}, and once to calibrate what this bed's instrument can hear
(\S\ref{sec:readout}).

\chapter{Baseline Diagnosis}
\label{ch:baseline}
% =========================================================================

\claim{Run as published, the official loop is an open loop measured through
a noisy instrument, prescribing from evidence that is wrong three times out
of four on the only subset anyone can verify --- and its one real repair
demonstrates that the machinery works when the signal is clean.}

This chapter reports what happened when the released self-evolving harness
was executed at scale without modification. Nothing in it depends on the
system built in Chapter~\ref{ch:ghx}: every finding here was established on
the no-graph baseline campaign, using scripts that read the loop's own raw
trajectories. That independence is deliberate. The diagnosis has to stand
before the treatment is described, or the treatment becomes the reason the
diagnosis was believed.

The chapter has one positive finding and three negative ones. The positive
finding --- \S\ref{sec:evidence-audit} --- is that the loop's central
evidence signal is structurally incapable of registering the thing it
claims to measure, and that this can be shown from the code and the bed
alone, before any data is examined. The negative findings are that feedback
is written but not delivered (\S\ref{sec:open-loop}), that the measurement
layer is contaminated from three directions (\S\ref{sec:measurement}), and
that the decision plane discards its own records (\S\ref{sec:decision}).
\S\ref{sec:one-repair} closes with the counter-example that keeps the
chapter honest: one repair in the whole campaign that demonstrably worked,
was demonstrably kept, and demonstrates that the retention machinery
engages when an effect is large enough to be seen.

\section{What the official loop does well, and where the release diverges}
\label{sec:official-strengths}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The diagnosis that follows is severe, and it would be misread without the
credit that precedes it. The released loop is the most carefully governed
self-evolving harness in the literature surveyed in Chapter~\ref{ch:related},
and six of its design choices are ones this thesis kept, built on, or
wished its neighbours had.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{It digests successes.} The Digester runs three templates ---
        all-fail, all-pass and partial --- and the all-pass template
        instructs it ``not to celebrate'' but to name the reusable strategy
        and any latent fragility. Most systems read only failures.
  \item \textbf{It invokes itself selectively.} Each round's actionability
        is computed mechanically from the dossier mix, and the evolve stage
        is skipped below a threshold. The loop does not edit for the sake
        of editing.
  \item \textbf{Every role runs behind scope gates.} No role may read the
        harness source or the history of another run, and the gate also
        catches absolute paths inside shell commands --- a defence against
        prompt injection and cross-experiment leakage that this thesis's
        own registry error 13 (\S\ref{sec:governance}) shows to be
        necessary.
  \item \textbf{Five deterministic gates, cheapest first.} A
        \emph{structure} gate enforces twelve evidence invariants --- every
        cited anchor must exist on disk and point inside its trajectory,
        manifests must carry their required keys, a candidate's bucket must
        match its file types, and lineage references must resolve. A
        \emph{novelty} gate hashes the file changes and refuses anything
        already in the ledger of refuted signatures. After
        canonicalization, a \emph{counterfactual} gate replays the proposed
        processor chain over recorded events from the previous round's
        passing tasks and rejects on any change to final output or exit
        reason --- no model call, no tool execution. A synthetic-task replay
        closes the ladder.
  \item \textbf{The Critic argues with the Evolver.} It judges each
        candidate against the configuration it would land in, demands
        round-trip evidence for tool and processor candidates, may
        interrogate the Evolver for up to two turns, and audits the
        candidates as a set against the scoreboard before any of them
        ships.
  \item \textbf{Shipped candidates are held to their own predictions.} Each
        names the tasks it expects to flip; the next round scores that
        prediction, and a hit rate below one half triggers rollback.
\end{enumerate}

Items 4 and 6 are the ancestors of this thesis's sixth gate and of its
localization metric respectively, and they are what made the released
system the right one on which to ask what such guards can see.

\subsection*{Where the release diverges from the paper}

Reproduction met five places where the code does not do what the paper
says (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} records each, with the code pins the
reproduction audit established):

\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{variant pool} and ensemble routing behind the paper's
        GAIA headline are absent --- the release is a single lineage with a
        \texttt{ship\,|\,no\_op} decision;
  \item the \emph{global ratchet} --- no solved task may be given back ---
        is not implemented; in its place stand an advisory watchlist over
        adjacent rounds, the counterfactual gate, and the hit-rate rollback
        (\S\ref{sec:open-loop});
  \item the $\pm 5\%$ \emph{noise threshold} on retention is not
        implemented; a three-state discretization and one sentence in the
        Critic's prompt approximate it;
  \item \emph{three seeds per cell} is the paper's table; the code defaults
        to $k{=}1$, rising to $k{=}2$ on a focus set;
  \item the Planner's per-candidate \emph{briefs} have been retired; it
        writes one landscape, and a single Evolver session chooses how many
        candidates to author.
\end{itemize}

What follows diagnoses the released system as fielded. Where a finding
depends on one of these divergences, the dependence is stated.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The system as fielded}
\label{sec:fielded}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The loop is the six-stage, four-role system of \S\ref{sec:motivation}:
solver batch, Digester dossiers, Planner landscape, Evolver candidates,
Critic verdict, five gates, and whatever lands becomes the configuration
the next round's solver runs under. Each round is bounded by a token and
cost budget and by a cap on the number of tasks attempted.

Two properties of this architecture matter for everything that follows.

The first is that \textbf{the loop edits itself through free text}. The
Evolver does not emit a typed patch; it composes a manifest and a
\texttt{config.yaml} as prose, and whatever survives that transcription is
the edit that actually lands. This is not a limitation of the edit
\emph{surface} --- the surface is wide, and \S\ref{sec:one-repair} shows
the loop installing an entirely new processor class through it --- but of
the edit's \emph{identity}. Nothing downstream can confirm that the
candidate document and the applied change describe the same intervention.

The second is that \textbf{the loop is a single lineage}. There is one
configuration, and it evolves round by round. There is no population, no
archive of alternatives, and --- despite what the paper specifies --- no
global ratchet guaranteeing that a task solved in round $k$ is still solved
in round $k+1$. A candidate that lands and is wrong has no floor beneath
it. \S\ref{sec:open-loop} shows what guards that position instead.

\section{Open-loop hazards: feedback written but not delivered}
\label{sec:open-loop}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

A self-evolving loop is only closed if the consequences of an edit reach
the author of the next edit. In the released system four separate channels
break that delivery. All four are code-pinned facts: they are properties of
the released tree, established by reading it, and none of them depends on
which campaign was flown or which model tier was configured.

\paragraph{The regression report is left empty by a round-indexing slip.}
The loop computes, each round, which previously-passing tasks have started
failing --- exactly the signal a lineage without a ratchet most needs. An
off-by-one in the round index means the comparison is drawn against an
empty set, so the report is structurally always empty. The authors' own
upstream fix has been adopted here, and every regression account in this
thesis is re-derived offline rather than read from the loop's output.

\paragraph{The verdict contract is stated twice and the two statements have
drifted apart.} The schema a role is instructed to emit and the schema the
consumer validates against no longer agree. Validation therefore fails
softly: the outcome is dropped rather than raised, and the next author is
never told what happened to the last edit.

\paragraph{The safety clamp cannot engage on a small bed.} Rollback
fires only when the pass \emph{rate} drops by at least $0.05$ \emph{and}
the pass \emph{count} drops by at least $3$ against the last validated
round (\texttt{run\_meta\_aegis.py:1425}). The conjunction is a property
of the code: wherever fewer than three tasks were passing, the count
condition cannot be met at all, and where exactly three were, only a
total loss meets it. The clamp is arithmetically unreachable in exactly
the situation --- new wiring, first exercised on a handful of tasks ---
for which a clamp exists.

\paragraph{A single lineage with no ratchet, guarded by a sentence.} The
paper specifies a global monotone ratchet: no previously solved task may be
given back. The code implements no such thing. What it has instead is an
advisory watchlist over adjacent rounds, which the agent may overrule by
declaring a regression transient; a counterfactual replay gate; and a
post-hoc rollback triggered when a shipped candidate's prediction hit rate
falls below $0.5$. None of these is a floor.

That this matters is not our inference. The released tree guards its own
worst known regression --- an Evolver edit that replaced the system-prompt
builder and thereby deleted a 23K-character injected domain policy,
recorded in the authors' own words as a ``catastrophic
regression\ldots dropped 55\%'' --- with an instruction inside a prompt
template rather than with a code path. And the only committed performance
number anywhere in the repository is a commit message recording that two
lineages \emph{never recovered} from one such crash \F{19}. A loop that
cannot un-poison its own lineage is the strongest argument this thesis can
make for attaching a readout to the retention decision, and
\S\ref{sec:future} makes it there.

\section{The measurement layer: three contamination sources}
\label{sec:measurement}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Before any claim can be read off this bed, three things have to be said
about the bed itself. Each was found by running it; each was hardened; and
every hardening was applied identically to both arms, so that none of them
can be a source of between-arm difference.

\paragraph{Judge false positives can flip the sign of a round.} Scoring is
performed by an LLM judge, and an unhardened judge's false positives are
large enough to reverse the sign of a round-over-round delta. The judge
used here is structurally hardened, and \emph{every} between-arm comparison
in this thesis uses audited scores only.

\paragraph{The bed's ground truth is partly on the public web.} Of a
44-task cohort assembled from the tasks that swing, twelve are
\emph{leak-route}: more than half of their passes touch benchmark
artifacts, and reference answers appear verbatim inside solver
trajectories \F{31}. The response was a leakage blocklist and a
decontamination census. The residual risk is disclosed in
\S\ref{sec:threats} rather than claimed away: both arms evaluate on the
same fixed bed every round, so whatever drift toward the bed exists is
shared, and the blocklist is the only held-out defence.

\paragraph{Re-measuring the same configuration moves 20\% of the bed.}
This is the finding that governs the design of every experiment in this
thesis, and it is stated here only to establish the problem; the arithmetic
is worked in \S\ref{sec:power}. Across the six ship-free windows of this
campaign's sixteen-round readout, $21.0\%$ of task outcomes flip between
two consecutive rounds running an identical configuration --- $126$ of
$600$ task-pairs --- and the resulting total-score swing runs from $-4$ to
$+5$ with no treatment applied at all \F{15}.

The third source is not a defect that hardening removes. It is a property
of the instrument, it replicates across three seeds
(\S\ref{sec:replication}), and it is the reason this thesis ends where it
does.

\section{The evidence-pipeline audit}
\label{sec:evidence-audit}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the chapter's positive finding, and the one on which
Chapters~\ref{ch:ghx} and \ref{ch:results} both depend.

Every dossier the Digester produces carries, for each tool call, a verdict
on whether the call's output was \emph{used}. That verdict is the loop's
only signal about whether an action mattered. It is consumed by the
Planner when ranking directions, quoted by the Evolver when justifying a
candidate, and reviewed by the Critic when deciding whether to veto. Across
the baseline campaign's sixteen scored rounds, the column judges only
$8.1\%$ of payload calls to have been used \F{1}. Something is either badly wrong with the harness or badly wrong with
the column.

It is the column, and the defect can be established in three tiers of
decreasing strength. \textbf{The thesis needs only the first two}, and
says so, because the third rests on a premise the data cannot supply.

\subsection*{(i) Construction}

The column tests whether the \emph{immediately next} assistant message
shares a verbatim substring of at least twenty characters with the tool
output (\texttt{harnessx/\allowbreak aegis/\allowbreak stages/\allowbreak trace\_facts.py}). Three kinds of
genuine use are therefore graded ``not referenced'' by construction, with
no data required to see it: use that is paraphrased rather than quoted; use
that occurs later than the next step; and use of any fact shorter than
twenty characters.

\subsection*{(ii) Blindness on this bed}

The third of those is not a corner case here. GAIA final answers have a
median length of \textbf{9} characters and a 90th percentile of
\textbf{14} \F{3} --- both below the column's own floor. A tool call that
retrieves a task's answer and hands it to a model that uses it correctly
produces, in the next assistant message, a string too short to clear the
threshold. \emph{On this bed, the column cannot register the use of an
answer.}

Tiers (i) and (ii) together settle the matter, and they settle it without
any experiment: the first is a property of the code, the second a property
of the benchmark. Neither depends on which arm was flown --- both were
established on the no-graph baseline --- and neither depends on the model
tier, since a twenty-character floor and a nine-character median answer are
unchanged by whichever model reads the digest.

\subsection*{(iii) Rate, and what it does not license}

The rate is reported because the reach of the defect matters, and it is
reported with its limit attached. Of the $559$ calls whose output literally
contains the final answer of a rollout that passed, \textbf{419
($75.0\%$)} are graded ``not referenced'', and $96.7\%$ of those carry an
answer below the column's own minimum length \F{2}. The figure is robust
across variants of the answer-length window at $73.8$--$75.1\%$ \F{2};
the column's own twenty-character threshold was not swept, and by tier (i)
it could not have been swept without changing the instrument.

\emph{The limit, stated plainly}: a call whose output contains the answer,
on a path that passed, is a call sitting on the successful path holding the
answer. That is not the same as having caused the pass. The finding
therefore rests on (i) and (ii), which need no causal premise, and the
$75.0\%$ is reported as a property of the column's output --- the rate at
which it applies its verdict to answer-carrying calls --- not as a count of
tool calls wrongly dismissed.

\subsection*{The disclaimer, and why it makes the case worse}

The Digester's own text does carry a caveat. It reads:

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Heuristic:} assistant message after the tool call does not contain
any $\ge$20-char substring from the tool output. This is the single most
common way a tool call ``fires but fails to affect the model'' --- e.g.\
when protocol flattening drops multimodal content.
\end{quote}

The disclaimer is the defect in miniature. It names one failure mode ---
multimodal flattening --- and never mentions that \emph{any answer under
twenty characters is invisible to it}, on a bed whose median answer is
nine. It describes the column as detecting calls that fire without
affecting the model, which is precisely the interpretation tier (ii)
forbids. And it does not travel with the verdict: the per-call rows in the
dossier's main table carry a bare \textbf{NO}.

\subsection*{Reach into decisions}

The verdict is not inert. It reaches \textbf{86.0\%} of dossiers \F{4}, it
is quoted verbatim inside three candidate documents \F{5}, and one
candidate was shipped on it \F{6}.

That shipped candidate named three tasks it expected to flip and hit none
of them. \emph{This is reported as illustration, not as inference.} Against
the round's base rate of $7/32$, a candidate with no effect whatever
returns zero of three with probability $0.48$. The specimen shows how a
false column propagated into a shipped decision; it says nothing about
whether that decision was good. The same evidentiary standard that
withdraws an $n{=}9$ comparison in \S\ref{sec:localization} applies with
more force at $n{=}1$, and is applied here.

\section{Decision-plane pathologies}
\label{sec:decision}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two further properties of the decision plane were recorded; both are
code-pinned.

\paragraph{The cause-of-death ledger is written and never read.} The loop
maintains a record of why each candidate died. Nothing consumes it. The
write sites and the absent read sites are both given in
Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}. This is the fourth broken delivery channel of
\S\ref{sec:open-loop}, and the most direct: the loop keeps a diary of its
own failures and does not open it.

\paragraph{The aimer is never shown where its aims landed.} The Planner
is structurally excluded from the loop's conversion priors --- the
guidance rebind list omits it (\texttt{planner.py:30--46}), and this
holds in every arm. The role that decides what to aim at is not shown
where previous aims landed, so whatever the loop learns about which
targets convert never reaches the stage that chooses targets.

\section{The one real repair}
\label{sec:one-repair}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Against all of the above, one intervention in the baseline campaign
demonstrably worked, and it is the most important single observation in
this thesis --- not because of its size, but because it is the only event
that calibrates the instrument.

The defect was mundane. A shell-quoting bug in the harness's Bash tool
caused commands to consume the round's token budget without producing
useful output, so tasks were dying of budget starvation rather than of
anything to do with their difficulty. The loop diagnosed this, authored a
guard processor, and shipped it. In the round that followed,
\texttt{budget\_exceeded} exits collapsed from $35$ to $4$; twelve of the
sixteen tasks that newly passed had died of starvation in the round before;
the level-2 subset gained $16.3$ percentage points; and the total score
rose by $9$ tasks, clearing the same-config band \F{39}. It is the largest
single-round move that campaign makes after its baseline draw, and --- of
the six above-band whole-bed moves in all six campaigns --- the only one
whose mechanism is legible in the exit ledger \F{48}.

\paragraph{Which number carries this section.} The mechanism endpoints,
with the $+9$ beside them rather than under them. The collapse from $35$ to
$4$ and the twelve-of-sixteen attribution identify the mechanism directly
and need no dispersion argument at all. The $+9$ agrees with them and
clears the same-config band.

That the band was cleared is, on its own, weaker evidence than it looks. A
sweep of every round pair in every campaign finds eleven above-band moves,
six of which occur in rounds where nothing shipped \F{48}; and among the
whole-bed rounds whose starvation count collapses by ten exits or more, the
score moves by $+9$, $+4$, $+2$ and $-3$ \F{49}. \emph{It is the mechanism
counts, not the band, that tie this move to this repair.} The band's role
here is narrower and still necessary: it is what makes the score effect
readable \emph{at all}, and the rest of this thesis is about how rarely
that happens.

\emph{The two calibers disagree about it, and the disagreement is stated
rather than resolved in this thesis's favour.} A range drawn from six
windows is not a standard deviation; the directly measured
$\sigma = 3.70$ of \S\ref{sec:power} places $+9$ inside three sigma. The repair
stands on the mechanism counts under either caliber. What the caliber
changes is only how loud an effect must be before the score axis hears it
--- and that question, not the repair, is the subject of this thesis.

\paragraph{How to read it.} This is an engineering-defect repair, not a
capability lift. The loop did not become better at solving GAIA tasks; it
stopped wasting the budget it needed to solve them. But it is also the
existence proof that the retention machinery is intact: the effect was
large enough to be seen, it was seen, and the drug was kept for the rest of
the campaign. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} returns to it twice --- once to show
that it never reached a later campaign, because the inheritance channel
does not exist \F{39}, and once because an intervention known to work is
the only thing that can tell us what this bed's instrument is able to hear
(\S\ref{sec:readout}).

\section{What the loop's evidence gets wrong}
\label{sec:evidence-wrong}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The first result is Chapter~\ref{ch:baseline}'s and is restated here only
to fix its place in the sequence. The loop's ``output used'' column cannot
register the use of a short fact, because it requires a twenty-character
verbatim overlap and this bed's answers have a median length of nine
\F{2}\F{3}. It applies its negative verdict to $75.0\%$ of the calls whose
output literally contains a passing rollout's answer \F{2}, that verdict
reaches $86.0\%$ of dossiers \F{4}, three candidate documents quote it
verbatim \F{5}, and one candidate was shipped on it \F{6}.

The sentence that closes the chapter is the one that licenses everything
after it: \emph{this finding does not require the graph}. It was
established on the no-graph arm, from the code and the bed.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{What the graph did, and what it did not}
\label{sec:what-graph-did}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{The false signal is gone}

Across the post-fix graph campaign, \textbf{0 of 859} dossiers carry the
misleading column, against a reach of $86.0\%$ in the no-graph arm
\F{7}\F{4}. This is the cleanest positive result in the thesis and also the
narrowest, for the reason given below.

\subsection*{Non-interference, by construction and by demonstration}

The recorder must not change the thing it records. Two arguments support
this, and they are of different kinds.

\emph{By construction}: the recorder is write-only with respect to the
loop's decision inputs, and every seam restores on exit
(\S\ref{sec:constraints}). Between-arm differences can flow only through
the loop's own decisions.

\emph{By demonstration}: a same-window paired trial on the stable cohort
--- eight always-pass canaries and two never-solved tasks, three
repetitions $\times$ two arms, with recording as the only difference ---
returns 9 of 10 per-task outcome vectors identical. The single divergence
is the documented flaky canary \F{33}, and it failed twice in the
recording-\emph{off} arm. Arm totals are $22/30$ against $24/30$: inside
noise, and signed \emph{against} interference \F{43}.

On a bed that flips one outcome in five (\S\ref{sec:power}), score identity
is not an achievable standard. Vector-level parity on stable tasks is, and
that is the standard used.

\subsection*{Removal is not measurement}

The replacement column saturates by construction under the sliding window:
$549/549$ \F{8}. The false signal's absence is verifiable; the
replacement's presence measures nothing. Any reading of
``$0/859$'' as evidence that the graph arm's tool calls were genuinely used
is unavailable, and is not made.

\subsection*{Citation integrity improves; the credit goes to the layer}

Digests in the graph arms carry \textbf{3.50} verified citation anchors
each, against \textbf{1.75} in the no-graph arms, while the share of
digests failing the production anchor validator falls from \textbf{11.7\%}
to \textbf{1.5\%} \F{52}. Roughly twice the citing, at roughly a seventh of
the failure rate --- so this is not the saturation trap of the previous
subsection, where cleanliness is bought by citing less.

\emph{Which component earns it is not established.} Three GHX changes could
each produce this: the anchor-repair pass, which is an independent flag;
the graph-fed digest itself; and the rebound digest builder. No arm
isolates them.

The defensible claim is therefore that \emph{the GHX layer} improves
citation integrity. That the \emph{graph} does is not shown. And because
the anchor-repair pass is separately switchable, this gain may be as
portable to an unmodified harness as the cost work of
\S\ref{sec:cost} --- which is to say, it may not be a graph result at all.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Downstream: outcomes do not separate; fewer candidates ship, and
they are cleaner}
\label{sec:localization}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{Localization, and why only one number from it survives}

The loop's own predictions carry real information. On the no-graph
baseline, the tasks a shipped candidate named flipped at \textbf{50.0\%}
against a base rate of \textbf{25.2\%} --- a lift of \textbf{1.99}, with a
95\% confidence interval of $[1.47, 2.54]$ by bootstrap over candidates (13
candidates, 50 eligible predictions), an interval that excludes $1.0$
\F{9a}\F{9d}.

\emph{That is the load-bearing localization number in this thesis}, and it
points the same way as the archaeology of \S\ref{sec:archaeology}: the base
loop can already aim. Prescription was never the bottleneck.

\subsection*{The between-arm comparison is withdrawn, not lost}

The graph arm's lift cannot be set beside it. The two shipped sets are
different candidate populations, admitted under different gate regimes ---
five gates against six, with $17\%$ killed against $57\%$ --- and drawn
against base pools nine percentage points apart. The graph arm's own
interval includes $1.0$ in any case, so even taken alone the value is
indistinguishable from chance.

Four stacked defects, and the superseded figures they retire, are recorded
in full at \F{9b}\F{9c}\F{9d}. The body takes no direction from them.

The design's reach is the honest closing number: a real \textbf{40\%}
improvement in localization would not have been visible at this sample
\F{9d}. This is an \emph{absent measurement}, not an underpowered null, and
the distinction is not pedantry --- an underpowered null still points
somewhere, and this one does not.

\subsection*{Outcomes}

Final scores do not separate. Ship rates are $0.88$ per round in the
no-graph arm against $0.62$ in the graph arm \F{10}. The switch
profit-and-loss account covers 21 items, none of which constitutes a measured
behavioural improvement \F{12}.

\subsection*{Fewer ships is not a regression}

The temptation is to read $0.62$ against $0.88$ as the loop getting worse.
The rest of the gate account says otherwise. Across the three graph seeds
the sixth gate killed \textbf{18} candidates that had cleared all five
official gates --- eighteen candidates caught naming a node that was never
there. And the share of candidates dying \emph{malformed} --- failing
canonicalization, replay and structure together --- fell from
\textbf{18/59 ($30.5\%$)} to \textbf{8/55 ($14.5\%$)}, the direct
consequence of both artifacts being machine-generated rather than
transcribed \F{53}.

Fewer candidates, of higher legality, behind one more gate. Whether that
trade is worth making is precisely what the outcome axis cannot resolve ---
which is the finding, not an evasion of it.

\subsection*{Why there is no component ablation}

A reader will ask for a level-by-level ablation of the switch ladder, and
the reason there is none needs to be the right reason.

It is \emph{not} that the control arm also rises. That fact rules out a
pure time trend and would not stop an ablation from being informative.

The reason is narrower. An ablation \emph{attributes} an arm-level effect
to a part, and on the score axis there is no arm-level effect to attribute:
the arms sit inside the same-config band (\S\ref{sec:power},
\S\ref{sec:replication}), so per-level arms would return the same ``not
separable'' verdict one level at a time, at $k$ times the cost.

Two secondary points are recorded as design costs rather than as
justifications. A level ablation on this bed would need its own seeds,
since one seed per level cannot resolve a difference inside the band. And
the contrast as flown is a flag \emph{bundle}, so a clean ladder would have
to re-fly every level on one bed --- a fixable expense, and one this
project did not pay.

What the thesis substitutes is bookkeeping, labelled \cls{C} observational
rather than presented as a causal decomposition: the per-edit localization
account \F{9a}\F{9b}\F{9c}, the four-layer mortality account \F{40}, the
ratchet's retention record \F{39}, and the reading that the gains both arms
show belong to a single mechanism family (\S\ref{sec:archaeology}).

The ablation that \emph{would} be decisive is not a level ablation of the
graph at all. It is the one \S\ref{sec:proposition} names: the readout,
attached to retention.

Both arm configurations were re-flown twice more, unchanged, for sixteen
rounds each. The replication target is not the score. It is the noise
geometry the readout argument stands on.

\subsection*{Operational deviations, disclosed in-body}

An unplanned machine restart killed one evolve stage and one task batch on
the second seed; the batch was re-flown from a byte-identical round-head
configuration, and the affected pair is read as a same-config pair. A
twelve-hour gateway outage on the third seed poisoned five rounds' fresh
rows, which were quarantined, the histories washed back to the clean
prefix, and both arms re-flown from the last verified round. Five
meta-chain holes occurred across the two third-seed arms, from the gateway
outage and from a binary-JSONL crash family in the evolve stage; pairs
that are not adjacent are left unpooled.

Both arms of both seeds completed sixteen of sixteen \emph{scored} rounds,
and every same-config window used in the flip statistics is a verified
clean pair \F{44}\F{45}.

\subsection*{The flip rate replicates}

The same-config flip rate is \textbf{20.1\%} on the second seed
($161/800$ pairs over eight full-batch windows, per-window swing
$-4\ldots{+}2$) against the first seed's \textbf{21.0\%} at the same
sixteen-round scope ($126/600$, $-4\ldots{+}5$) --- near-identical to the
pair. The third seed lands at \textbf{19.2\%} ($115/600$ over six windows,
swing $-8\ldots{+}5$) \F{15}\F{44}\F{45}. All three are computed on the
same 100-task bed, so the comparison is between seeds and nothing else.

Three seeds: $21.0$ / $20.1$ / $19.2$. This is the number the readout
argument needs, and it is stable.

\subsection*{The volatile family replicates too}

Flip noise is not uniform across the bed. Tasks that flipped within the
last three rounds re-flip at \textbf{41.6\%} on the first seed,
\textbf{37.7\%} on the second ($49/130$ over five windows), and
\textbf{31.5\%} on the third ($51/162$, six windows) --- $1.6$ to
$2.1\times$ each seed's own bed rate \F{41}\F{44}\F{45}.

The flip concentration that makes single-window readings unreadable is
therefore a structural property of the bed, not one campaign's draw.

\subsection*{Both arms climb; the climb is the one thing three seeds
settle}
\label{sec:trend}

Fitting a line to rounds R1--R15 on the common 100-task subset gives a
\textbf{positive slope in all six campaigns} --- no-graph $+0.62$, $+0.44$,
$+0.38$ tasks per round; graph $+0.53$, $+1.39$, $+0.64$. Under a null of
no trend, six positives out of six is a $1/64$ event, $p \approx 0.016$
\F{47}. \emph{The loop genuinely improves within a campaign}, and it does
so in both arms.

The endpoint difference a reader reaches for first --- round 0 to terminal
--- is \emph{not} that finding. Across the six arms it correlates with
where round 0 happened to land at $r = -0.87$ \F{46}. That is regression to
the mean on a bed whose same-config swing is $-8\ldots{+}5$: the arms that
``improved most'' are the arms whose first round fell lowest.

Between arms, on the common bed: plateau means over R3--R15 are
\textbf{63.4} tasks (no-graph) against \textbf{66.1} (graph), a gap of
$+2.7$ \F{46}; the within-campaign slope gap is $+0.37$ tasks per round at
an exact permutation $p = 0.10$; and the last four rounds differ by $+5.25$
at $p = 0.15$ \F{47}.

\emph{Every one of those sits inside the band, and the last number in the
paragraph is the one that matters}: with three seeds per arm, the smallest
attainable one-sided permutation $p$ is $0.05$. An observed $p$ of $0.10$
is one exchange away from the design's floor. \textbf{``Not significant''
here does not mean ``no effect''; it means the design reached its own
ceiling before it reached significance.} The loop improves; whether the
graph is what makes it improve is a question this data cannot answer in
either direction.

\section{The space, re-scoped}
\label{sec:respace}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

Measuring the bed changed what counts as a reasonable target on it.

\paragraph{Ceilings.} Of the 100 tasks, 97 are reachable at all; 3 are
never solved; 47 are reachable but unreliable, passing under $80\%$ of the
time. The union of everything ever solved, 97, exceeds the best single
round any arm recorded, 76, by \textbf{21} tasks \F{16}. A loop optimizing a single round's score is optimizing a
number 25 below what its own history has already achieved.

\paragraph{Where the variance is injected.} Twin-trajectory analysis puts
$74\%$ of divergence at a plan fork (upper bound) and $14\%$ at an
environment-first difference (lower bound), with the median divergence at
the second call \F{22}. Empty tool results are non-predictive of outcome
(9 against 8; an arbiter preferring fewer empties is a coin flip) \F{23},
and arbiter feasibility is one strong, one weak, one reversed \F{24}. At
content level, pure sampling accounts for about $17\%$; about $80\%$ of
plan forks follow a world difference rather than precede it; and the
injection point is a web search in 76 of 79 cases \F{25}.

That last number is actionable and \S\ref{sec:future} acts on it: the
measured variance injection point is a cache away from being closed.

\section{The clinic: capability against efficacy}
\label{sec:clinic}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

The campaigns answer whether the graph changes outcomes. They do not answer
whether the loop is \emph{able} to diagnose, because a campaign confounds
ability with everything else in the loop. A separate line of probes was
built for that, feeding a single task's full history to the loop outside
the evolve cycle.

\subsection*{The capability chain is intact end to end}

$k{=}1$ observations suffice for the loop to reach the correct drug family.
More trajectories buy argument quality, claim-narrowing and honest
abstention --- not new families \F{26}--\F{30}. Fed a cluster of related
failures, the loop returns one general prescription, narrows its own claim,
and abstains where it should \F{36}.

Pushed with a coverage directive --- treat every task, never abstain ---
the loop's rational optimum is to engineer the bed's own leak routes into a
benchmark-lookup cheat tool, which passed the Critic and \emph{all} landing
gates \F{37}. It was not shipped for scoring; the text evidence is
retained. \S\ref{sec:governance} reads it as the governance result it is.

\emph{Discipline sentence for this subsection}: every item above is
capability evidence. None of it is efficacy evidence. The distinction is
the chapter's spine.

\subsection*{Efficacy is zero}

Three drugs the loop repeatedly prescribes were assembled into a
configuration bundle and run through the pre-registered trial protocol of
\S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol}.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Clean-cluster transfer: $+1$ and $+2$, inside the $\pm3$ floor ---
        engaged but unreadable \F{32}.
  \item Canary set: $23/24$ against $24/24$; at worst harmless \F{33}.
  \item \textbf{Pre-registered verdict: bundle effect $= 0$} \F{34}. The
        two readings are kept apart. The prospective batch --- 7 clean
        tasks $\times$ 10 repetitions $\times$ 2 arms, $n{=}70$ pairs ---
        returns $+1/-4$, sign test $p = 0.97$. The pooled clean
        same-window pairs ($n{=}174$) return 75 for the parent against 72
        for the applied configuration, one-sided Fisher $p = 0.67$. And
        the mechanism endpoint, confident-wrong share, is flat in both.
\end{itemize}

Scores flat with mechanism flat is a true zero, not a resolution failure
(\S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol}). This is the one confirmatory test in the
thesis and it returned nothing.

\subsection*{The execution history of four apparent wins}

The route to that verdict is worth recording, because it is the clearest
demonstration in this document of why the protocol has the shape it does.
Four successive apparent wins were killed by three different instruments:
the first by firing forensics --- the score gains sat entirely on
repetitions where the drug never fired, 0 of 5 \F{36}; the second by a
same-window control returning $6/10$ against $6/10$ \F{36}; and the last by
the pre-registered prospective batch \F{34}.

\subsection*{Pathology census}

Of the 44 swing tasks: 12 are leak-route, 13 sit at a capability wall, 16
are wrong-closure failures, 2 are friction, and 1 is starvation. The
quarantine bin verified 5 of 5 \F{31}.

The census matters because it prices the remaining work. Only one of those
five families --- starvation --- was ever repaired, and the family that
dominates, wrong-closure, needs an organ the loop does not have
(\S\ref{sec:future}).

\section{Archaeology, the treadmill, and four layers of mortality}
\label{sec:archaeology}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection*{The loop had already prescribed correctly, four campaigns
earlier}

The finding that reorients the thesis came from re-reading our own
archives, and it falsified our own earlier claim.

Three ancestors, with their decision quotes \F{38}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item In the \emph{zero-graph} baseline arm, round 3, a prompt repair
        carrying a step-cited fabrication diagnosis --- the loop had
        identified a specific fabricated identifier absent from every tool
        output --- accepted ``first in ranking''. This is four campaigns
        before the dossier mechanism that was supposed to make such
        diagnosis possible.
  \item In the graph campaign, round 1, a dormant-rule activation shipping
        with replay counts of 13 fires and 5 false positives on one side,
        9 and 4 on the other; accepted, ship-alone.
  \item An archive-fetch tool, invented by the loop itself.
\end{itemize}

Each family re-surfaces in later campaigns and probes \emph{without
inheriting} from the earlier instance. This is the \textbf{re-prescription
treadmill}: the same drug, re-derived from scratch, repeatedly.

\subsection*{Card anatomy: what the graph actually changed}

The no-graph cards already reason from symptom to mechanism to drug, using
step-pinned quotes from the trajectory --- a fabricated identifier absent
from every tool output, a zero-length return, the specific prompt line that
sanctioned the behaviour. \emph{Diagnosis is native to the base loop.}

What the graph changes is the evidence \emph{regime}. The graph-arm card
replays its proposed rule over all 100 recorded execution graphs and ships
with fire \emph{and false-positive} counts, wiring citations, an explicit
self-grading of what its evidence does not show, and a priced interaction
with a concurrent candidate \F{38}.

Argument quality climbs to readout level 2. And level 2 is still not level
3: measured firing sites are death signatures, not cure sites. The
localization axis that would have settled the difference is not measurable
on this design (\S\ref{sec:localization}).

\subsection*{The one drug that worked, and where it went}

The budget-starvation repair of \S\ref{sec:one-repair} was retained from
round 3 to round 16 of its own campaign --- the ratchet's existence proof
--- and then reached no later campaign configuration examined \F{39}.

The mechanism is decisive independently of how many campaigns are grepped:
the drug's target is a \texttt{file://} absolute path into its own campaign
directory, and every later seed is generated from the clean template.
\emph{The inheritance channel does not exist.} The loop did not forget the
drug; it was never able to pass it on.

\subsection*{The gain face: what else clears the band, and what clearing it
             means}
\label{sec:gain-face}

The positive control is one round pair. Reported alone it invites a reading
it does not support --- that above-band moves are edits and inside-band
moves are noise --- so the whole gain face was swept: all $84$
round-to-round pairs that follow the six campaigns' baseline draws, on the
common 100-task bed, against the same-config band $-8\ldots{+}5$ \F{48}.
Round 0 is excluded exactly as in \S\ref{sec:trend}: it is a single draw,
it anchors the regression-to-the-mean artifact, and on the first seed it is
the one round scored at $k{=}2$.

Eleven pairs leave the band, all of them upward. \textbf{Six of the eleven
land in rounds where nothing shipped at all.} Five of those six are no-op
audit windows, where only 25 tasks are re-drawn and the delta is a partial
re-draw rather than a round-to-round move; restricting to pairs whose two
rounds both re-ran the whole bed leaves six above-band moves, five
ship-bearing and one not. The five carry candidates from three different
buckets, share no mechanism, and only one of them --- the positive control
--- has a mechanism legible in the exit ledger. The count is
caliber-sensitive in the expected direction, and not in a way that rescues
it: under
the $\pm 7$ single-pair ceiling of \F{14} the whole-bed above-band set is
three, all ship-bearing.

\emph{Clearing the band is therefore not sufficient evidence that an edit
worked.} Six of eleven had no edit. What follows shows it is not necessary
either.

intervention's size without reference to the score. Four whole-bed round
pairs collapse it by ten exits or more (Table~\ref{tab:starvation})

The honest caveat comes first. A starvation collapse is not \emph{obliged}
to raise the score: a task that stops running out of steps starts
answering, and its answer may be wrong. The four rows are consistent with a
mechanism that converts starvation failures into wrong-answer failures at a
rate this bed does not fix, and this thesis does not exclude that reading
\cls{C}.

It does not rescue the axis either way. Under any reading, an intervention
whose mechanical size is measured, large, and identical across two
campaigns produced score movements landing on opposite sides of the noise
band. A signal with that property cannot rank edits by how much they
helped, and ranking edits by how much they helped is the only thing a
selection signal is for.

\subsection*{Four layers of mortality} \cls{C}

Why a loop that can prescribe correctly still does not improve, in four
layers \F{40}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Placebo at the capability wall} (primary). The drug is
        correct and the lesion is out of the actuator's reach.
  \item \textbf{Lifetime of at most one campaign.} Seed restarts, the
        three-tier veto, prompt singleton overwrites.
  \item \textbf{Shipped is not firing.} Recorder-gated dormancy, merge
        drops, landing bugs.
  \item \textbf{No readout, therefore no ratchet.}
\end{enumerate}

The fourth layer catches the first three. Without an efficacy readout the
loop does not know that a drug was ineffective --- so it re-prescribes the
placebo; does not know that a drug was lost --- so it does not repair
inheritance; and does not know that a drug went silent --- so it does not
check the wiring.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The survivor}
\label{sec:survivor}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

One positive channel survived the whole study. Episodic notes ---
answer-scrubbed per-task history, prepended directly to the task and
bypassing the evolve loop entirely --- returned $22/72$ against a bare
$17/72$: $+5$, with 3 tasks gained and 1 lost, not significant \F{35}. The
largest gains fell where the note was most on-target. Deciding its fate
needs three or four more window pairs, and it is reported as honestly
unresolved.

Its interest is structural rather than numerical. It is the only channel
that bypasses all four mortality layers of \S\ref{sec:archaeology}: it does
not occupy the prompt singleton slot, because it is injected per task; it
does not pass the governance gates, because it never enters the evolve
loop; it cannot go dormant, because it is prepended to the task itself; and
it does not need the loop to read an efficacy signal, because the operator
decides its life or death.

The price of that immunity is that it is no longer self-evolution. It is
retrieval-based experience reuse --- which is exactly the adjacent
literature line \S\ref{sec:graph-memory} sets aside, and the boundary is
worth noticing: the one thing that worked is the one thing that gave up the
property under study.

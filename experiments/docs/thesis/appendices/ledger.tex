\chapter{Fact Ledger}
\label{app:ledger}
% =========================================================================

Every number this thesis measures carries an \F{} anchor into this
ledger; the abstract restates body figures without anchors, and figures
transcribed from cited literature carry their citation instead. Each
row states the fact, its value, the \emph{sample and scope} it was computed
over, and its source class:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=3em,style=nextline]
  \item[\textnormal{\cls{A}}] \textbf{Recomputable.} A named script
        regenerates the number from raw run artifacts. May be stated
        directly in the body.
  \item[\textnormal{\cls{B}}] \textbf{Recorded.} Observed and logged, but
        not regenerable from a script --- typically an operational event.
        Must be written as ``recorded at\ldots'' at its point of use.
  \item[\textnormal{\cls{C}}] \textbf{Judgment.} An interpretation. Must be
        labelled as judgment at its point of use.
\end{description}

Two rules govern the ledger and are stated here because they constrain the
whole document. \emph{Sample and scope must appear in the same paragraph as
the number}, so that no figure can be quoted away from the population it
was computed over. And \emph{no number enters the thesis before its ledger
row and its recompute script both exist} --- a rule this project adopted
after discovering that several of its own early headline figures could not
be regenerated.

Script paths are relative to \texttt{experiments/analysis/} unless given in
full. Class-A recomputation is conditional on restoring separately supplied
archives under recorded legacy directory names or repointing machine-local
roots. This applies to \texttt{audit\_\allowbreak candidate\_\allowbreak prediction\_\allowbreak lift\_\allowbreak whitelist.py},
\texttt{proof\_\allowbreak stats.py}, \texttt{pathology\_\allowbreak census.py},
\texttt{majority\_\allowbreak at\_\allowbreak k\_\allowbreak sim.py}, \texttt{cone\_\allowbreak size\_\allowbreak recompute\_\allowbreak m26b.py}
and \texttt{audit\_\allowbreak m26b\_\allowbreak noop\_\allowbreak flip\_\allowbreak rate.py}; the proof script may skip
missing batch files, so completeness of the restored layout must be checked. Where a row's scope was narrowed by the whitelist ruling
(\S\ref{sec:scope}), the row states the narrowed scope; superseded readings
are not reproduced. \textbf{F17},
\textbf{F27} and \textbf{F28}
are supporting or archival rows: measured and recorded, cross-checking other rows or reporting a
probe the body does not need, and not meant for standalone citation. Four
numbers (F18, F23, F24 and F50) are unassigned: their rows were removed in
revision and the numbering was kept stable so that no other anchor moved.

\item[\textbf{F9c}\quad Why F9a and F9b are not an arm comparison.]
Five compounding defects. \emph{(i)} the arms' 22 shipped candidates are
22 \emph{different} edits, not one treatment twice. \emph{(ii)} base
pool $25.2\%$ against $34.2\%$, so equal hit rates would already give
unequal lift. \emph{(iii)} five gates against six, the sixth testing
whether a cited graph node exists --- \emph{unrelated to predictive
accuracy} --- thinning the sample. \emph{(iv)} the graph arm moves
$0.78 \to 1.23$, $n: 9 \to 2$, across readings. \emph{(v)} graph-arm core/halo labels cover a broader prediction
population than the declared unlock lists and omit registry-tool touches
\F{64}. \emph{Conclusion:} the localization face is
\textbf{not adjudicable}. \emph{Class:} \cls{A} for the components,
\cls{C} for the conclusion.

\item[\textbf{F9d}\quad Uncertainty on the lift estimates.]
\emph{Archived diagnostic, not calibrated inferential support.} The
candidate-cluster bootstrap used 20{,}000 resamples and returned no-graph
$1.99$, $95\%$ interval $[1.47, 2.54]$, design effect $0.93$, and graph
$0.78$, $95\%$ interval $[0.34, 1.23]$, design effect $1.50$. Candidate rows

minimum cluster count (three possible resamples; design effect $0.10$). The
nominal MDEs --- no-graph $1.51$, graph $1.37$ unadjusted and $\approx 1.46$
adjusted --- are model-based one-sided exact-binomial calculations at
$\alpha=0.05$ against each arm's own base pool, not calibrated power for
the flown design. \emph{Class:} \cls{A},
\texttt{audit\_lift\_uncertainty.py}, with a self-check gate that
reproduces the point estimates from the independent script before printing
an interval.

\item[\textbf{F34}\quad Efficacy verdict on the pre-specified test.]
Prospective batch (7 clean tasks $\times 10 \times 2$, same window):
\textbf{$+1/-4$, sign test $p = 0.97$} (parent 29/70, applied 21/70).
Repetitions are aggregated into seven task-level differences; five non-tied
signs enter the test. With five signs the one-sided test rejects only when
all five agree ($p = 0.031$), so it can detect only an effect that improves
nearly every task: 80\% power needs a per-task improvement probability of
about $0.96$.
Pooling every clean same-window pair, \textbf{174 pairs: parent 75/174
against applied 72/174} (Fisher one-sided $0.67$), retained descriptively
because it ignores pairing and task clustering. Mechanism endpoint
also flat: confident-wrong among failures 51/99 against 54/102. A stopping
computation showed the second batch could not reverse the result, so it was
cancelled. \textbf{Verdict: no score improvement, with a negative point estimate}
(parent 29/70 against applied 21/70 prospectively, 75/174 against 72/174
pooled) --- and the preceding four apparent wins did not survive their
respective checks. \emph{Single-window reading convention for this bed:} a
single task's base rate swings $\pm 50$ points on a half-hour scale, and a
single-window gain of up to five tasks is insufficient, by itself, to
attribute an improvement. \emph{Sample:} 174 execution pairs, the
two leakage-cluster tasks excluded; the confirmatory analysis unit is the
task-level aggregate stated above. \emph{Record:} rules written in the
working notes before launch, committed together with the verdict; see
\S\ref{sec:efficacy-protocol} and \texttt{docs/provenance/}. \emph{Class:} \cls{A},
\texttt{proof\_stats.py}.

\item[\textbf{F60}\quad Spearman--Brown repetition price on the flip rate.]
Modelling each task's repeated same-config draws as independent Bernoulli
trials on its own pass propensity (the two-rater, equal-marginals identity
behind Scott's $\pi$ / Fleiss's $\kappa$: disagreement $f = 2\bar p(1-\bar
p)(1-r_1)$) recovers a single-draw reliability $r_1 = 0.568$ from F9e's
flip rate ($f=20.10\%$) and the same population's marginal pass rate
($\bar p = 63.25\%$, $2{,}530/4{,}000$ cells). Spearman--Brown then prices
repetition: $k{=}2$ averaged draws give $r_2=0.724$, $k{=}3$ gives
$r_3=0.798$, and \textbf{$k{=}4$ is the first repetition count reaching
a conventional $0.80$ reliability bar} ($r_4=0.840$); that bar is a
reliability coefficient, not the $80\%$ pass-rate threshold of F16; the
fixed-propensity model is a simplification (F41's propensities concentrate
in a volatile family and shift across windows).
\emph{Sample:} as F9e --- three no-graph seeds, same windows, common
100-task subset. \emph{Class:} \cls{A}, \texttt{audit\_spearman\_brown.py}.

\item[\textbf{F70}\quad Same-instant re-measurement, and what this design
      can resolve.]
The baseline campaign's round 0 scored every task at $k{=}2$ --- two
rollouts issued in one batch under one configuration --- and it is the only
same-instant whole-bed re-measurement in the six campaigns. On the common
100-task subset the two simultaneous draws score \textbf{61} and
\textbf{57}: \textbf{a swing of four tasks with time held fixed}.
\textbf{$16.0\%$} of tasks (16/100) disagree between them, 95\% Wilson
$[10.1, 24.4]$. \textbf{This does not separate from the adjacent-window
rate}: F15's $21.0\%$ $[17.9, 24.4]$ and F9e's pooled $20.1\%$ $[18.4,
21.9]$ both lie inside it. \emph{One window, $n=1$}: the interval is wide,
no between-window drift component is resolved, and none is claimed absent.
The supported reading is the weaker one --- the re-measurement noise of
F15/F9e is \emph{consistent with} being entirely same-instant.
\textbf{Conditional sensitivity model:} assume conditionally independent
task-level evaluation errors and independent repeated Bernoulli draws at fixed
task propensities, using the same variance model in both arms. The graph arm
has no whole-bed same-configuration twin window, so equality of that variance
model across arms is untested. Under those assumptions the simultaneous rate gives a per-window score SD of
$\sqrt{100\times0.080}=2.83$ tasks and a predicted paired-change SD of
$4.00$; F15 directly observes $3.70$ over 20 windows (SE $0.60$).
The model gives an 80\%-power MDE of $11.2/\sqrt{k}$ tasks --- $11.2$,
$5.6$ and $2.8$ at $k=1,4,16$ --- and maps F46's $+2.7$ to $k\approx17$.
\textbf{Where the flown design's samples went:} the no-graph arm logged 39
whole-bed plateau rounds; the graph arm logged 24, plus 15 25-task audit
batches with carried outcomes (F58). If 39 rounds were independent repeats of
one fixed configuration under the model, the MDE would be $1.8$ tasks; F46's
between-seed calculation is $10.4$. The campaigns evolved configurations and
did not provide those repeats. The quantities above show resolution available
under the sensitivity model, not an identified loss under the flown dependence
structure.
\emph{Sample:} baseline seed 1, round 0, fresh rows, common 100-task
subset; no other campaign logs a $k \ge 2$ round. \emph{Class:} \cls{A},
\texttt{audit\_simultaneous\_twins.py}; the resolution arithmetic is
arithmetic on this row with F15 and F46, not a new measurement.

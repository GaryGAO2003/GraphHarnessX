% =========================================================================
\chapter{Methodological Deviation Register}
\label{app:deviations}
% =========================================================================

Every departure from the protocol the official paper describes, with its
pre-declared fallback where one existed and its effect on claim strength.
The register exists so that a reader can reconstruct what was changed and
decide independently whether the change mattered; entries are listed even
where the effect is judged to be none.

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{A.1\quad The evidence-scope ruling}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

One evidence-whitelist ruling narrowed what this thesis is allowed to claim.
It is dated and its direction is recorded because the ruling was made after
the excluded campaigns had run and been read.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The evidence whitelist} (2026-08-26). Two graph campaigns
        flown before the graph-layer fixes committed between the second
        shakedown and the first whitelist graph campaign (19--26 August
        2026), each pinned by a regression test, are excluded from the claim-bearing campaign evidence, as are all runs
        predating the baseline campaign. The ruling was made
        \emph{after} those campaigns had run and been read, and it moved the
        headline numbers \emph{against} the graph: the arm-comparison of
        localization lift that it invalidated was the project's most
        graph-favourable reading. The sole exception is one earlier partial-graph
        candidate-and-decision artifact retained as a labelled archival specimen
        in \S\ref{sec:archaeology} and F38, supporting retrospective proposal
        capability only; its firing, outcome and localization measurements remain
        excluded from campaign results and comparisons.
\end{itemize}

\section*{A.2\quad Deviations from the published protocol}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,style=nextline,itemsep=5pt]

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{Paper-versus-release divergences.}}] Five
        places where the released code does not do what the paper says,
        established by reading the release (\S\ref{sec:motivation},
        \S\ref{sec:official-strengths}). \emph{(i)} The variant pool and
        ensemble routing behind the paper's GAIA headline are absent: no
        \texttt{variant}, \texttt{ensemble}, \texttt{fork} or
        \texttt{retire} path exists in any branch, the orchestrator
        describes itself as a six-stage loop, and its \texttt{num\_evolvers}
        field is annotated as no longer dispatching. \emph{(ii)} The global
        ratchet is not implemented; the release substitutes an advisory
        watchlist over adjacent rounds, the counterfactual gate, and the
        post-round rollback rule at \texttt{run\_meta\_aegis.py:1425}
        (\S\ref{sec:open-loop}). \emph{(iii)} The $\pm 5\%$ noise threshold
        is not implemented (next item but one). \emph{(iv)} Three seeds per
        cell is the paper's table; the code defaults to $k{=}1$ and rises
        to $k{=}2$ only on a focus set. \emph{(v)} The Planner's
        per-candidate briefs are retired; it writes one landscape and a
        single Evolver session chooses how many candidates to author. The
        GAIA runner additionally defaults to a single task
        (\texttt{MAX\_TASKS\_DEFAULT=1}) and the repository's
        \texttt{.gitignore} excludes run outputs, which is why no GAIA
        number in the release is recomputable \F{19}.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{Model tier.}}] \begingroup\sloppy
        \texttt{deepseek-\allowbreak v4-\allowbreak flash} on the solver,
        \texttt{deepseek-\allowbreak v4-\allowbreak pro} on the four meta roles and the judge,
        in every campaign, against the paper's frontier meta tier on a
        different bed (\S\ref{sec:beds}). \emph{Effect on claim strength:} the solver--meta-model pair is held fixed
        across the two arms and therefore does not by itself confound their
        internal comparison; generalization beyond that pair is untested.\par\endgroup

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{Edit-surface coverage.}}] The typed candidate
        surface covers the processor plane, two configuration sections and
        the prompt templates. Editing the harness's own source is out of
        scope (\S\ref{sec:candidate-surface}). \emph{Effect:} the typed surface is strictly \emph{narrower} than the official free-text
        one. It can reduce attainable behavioural benefit while mechanically
        favouring legality or malformed-candidate endpoints by excluding
        unsupported edit forms; the study does not identify one direction
        across endpoints.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{The registered L5 deviation.}}] One rung of the
        switch ladder modifies official prompt content --- the only rung
        that does. It is registered because it is a named confound for any
        behavioural difference that would otherwise be attributed to
        evidence rather than to instruction.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{Round indexing.}}] Round~0 is a pure baseline
        round and does not enter the engine; adaptation begins at round~1,
        so $N$ configured rounds yield $N-1$ adaptation rounds. This is a
        consequence of the design in which the Digester reads the previous
        round's ledgers --- round~0 must seed them. Every window in this
        thesis is stated in the sixteen-round convention, and round~0 is
        excluded from every trend and gain-face reading
        (\S\ref{sec:trend}, \S\ref{sec:gain-face}).

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{pass@1 throughout.}}] The paper's main
        evaluation is pass@2. Every score in this thesis is pass@1, and
        mixed-$k$ passes do not count, so every round is read on one
        scored attempt per task (\S\ref{sec:evaluator-bias}).
        \emph{Effect:} one attempt-count convention is used across rounds and
        campaigns; it does not equalize the arms' measurement policies.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{The $\pm 5\%$ noise threshold.}}] The paper
        tabulates an ignored single-round delta of $\pm 5\%$ without saying
        where it applies. It is adopted here as an \emph{analysis-layer}
        reading only: a single-round aggregate move inside the band carries
        no directional claim. The per-task gate is unchanged. The runner's
        own post-round rollback rule --- revert the round's ships when the
        rate falls by $5$ points \emph{and} the count by $3$ tasks
        (\texttt{run\_meta\_aegis.py:1425}) --- is a separate,
        vendored mechanism and was left exactly as the released code has
        it.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{Single lineage.}}] No campaign in this thesis
        records a fork event, so the variant pool holds one variant
        throughout. The paper's routing, forking and retirement machinery
        is therefore never exercised, and no claim is made about it. Every
        result here concerns a single evolving configuration.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{Manifest schema and capability evidence.}}]
        \cls{B} The paper's change-manifest contract does not exist in the
        open repository, whose meta-agent writes journal vocabulary
        instead; paper-only fields are marked missing rather than
        fabricated. Where the meta roles author no capability evidence, the
        recipe measures the candidate's real tool output through the
        production serializer and passes or fails the gate on that
        measurement. Both are recorded in the project's
        paper-versus-reimplementation ledger, which is maintained
        separately and covers
        in addition the Ensemble machinery this thesis does not exercise.

  \item[\textnormal{\textbf{The efficacy trial's \texttt{-{}-max-steps}
        correction.}}] The loader default of 40 doubles the campaign
        step budget and revives burnout tasks; the trial arms were
        re-flown under the campaign cap of 20, which both arms share.

\end{description}
